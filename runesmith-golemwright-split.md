## Runesmith
Runesmiths are scribes of power who believe a word is worth more written down than spoken aloud. They carve spells into stone, steel and parchment, leave traps on doorframes, and hand their friends runes to break in an emergency. In a fight, they're slower than other casters, but their spells can't be heard coming.

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light and simple weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 1 rank to Change, 1 to Reveal, and 1 to any school of your choice.

**Lettered.** Your mundane Reveal actions to read, decipher or spot writing, symbols and traps have a mortal limit of 4.

### Written Word
You can cast spells by writing them instead of speaking them. Whenever you cast a spell, you can choose to write it.

- **What you need.** A free hand and something to write on. A quill and parchment works, but so does a finger in the dust or a knife on a tabletop.
- **Silent.** A written spell makes no sound, unless the DM decides it does, such as when you carve it into stone. Either way, it can't be countered with the Prepare action, and it works while you're silenced or gagged. If it does make a sound, it can still give away your position. Its range is the same as if it was spoken.
- **Slower.** Writing takes longer than speaking. A written spell takes effect at the end of the round, after every casting turn. If you're downed before then, the spell is left unfinished and doesn't work.

\column

### Runes
Instead of casting a written spell right away, you can leave it as a rune on a surface or object you can touch. When you write it, choose how it's triggered:

- **Released.** Whoever holds the object can release the spell as a bonus action. This lets you hand runes to your allies.
- **Set.** The spell takes effect when something happens, such as the rune being touched, read, or stepped over.

A rune fades once its spell takes effect, or after your next long rest if it hasn't. You can have a number of runes at once shown in the Runesmith table. Runes are hard to spot: a rune has a concealment level equal to the level of the spell it holds (see *Passive Reveal*).

You can also write runes outside of combat, rolling a pre-turn and spelling them as normal. Because you can take your time and try again, runes you write outside of combat hold spells up to level 2.

### Level 1: Drafting
Writing gives you time to think. When you write a spell, you can swap 1 of your tiles for a random tile from the pile before you reveal it.

### Level 3: Thrown Ink
You can write spells and runes on surfaces and objects you can see within 30 feet, instead of only ones you can touch.

### Level 6: Deep Runes
Runes you write outside of combat can hold spells up to level 4.

### Level 9: Fluent Hand
Your written spells now take effect on your casting turn, like spoken spells. You can still choose to have one take effect at the end of the round instead, such as to wait for an enemy to step into place.

### Level 12: Grand Inscription
Runes you write outside of combat can hold spells up to your rank. Once per long rest, you can release or trigger all your runes on a single casting turn.

### Runesmith Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Shorthand.** A written spell of 4 letters or fewer takes effect on your casting turn, like a spoken spell.
- **Trigger Word.** As a bonus action, you can trigger any of your runes within 60 feet of you, whatever trigger you chose for it.
- **Rune Ammunition (4+).** A rune you've put on a weapon is released for free when that weapon's attack hits.
- **Deep Carving (4+).** Your runes can each be used twice before they fade.
- **Living Workshop (8+).** You can have 2 more runes at once. Your runes don't fade after a long rest, and when one is used, it recharges after your next long rest instead of fading.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Runewarden
Runewardens turn rooms into traps and battlefields into puzzles. Few things walk out of a place they've prepared.

- **Level 2: Lasting Runes.** You can have 1 more rune at once. Your runes don't fade after a long rest, and last for a week instead.
- **Level 5: Chain Runes.** When one of your set runes is triggered, you can trigger another of your runes within 30 feet of it.
- **Level 10: Warding Circle.** Once per long rest, as your action, you can draw a circle of runes up to 30 feet across around you, wherever you are. For the next hour, enemy spells cast inside the circle or into it take effect 1 level lower, and you can have up to 3 extra runes inside it. The circle is hard to spot: it has a concealment level equal to your Change rank, so enemies only notice it if their passive Reveal equals or beats it, or if they search for it.

#### Alchemist
Alchemists write their spells into potions instead of objects, brewing remedies and poisons for every occasion.

- **Level 2: Bottled Words.** During a long rest, roll your Change casting pool, draw tiles as normal, and spell up to as many words as your Change rank. Each word becomes a potion that holds that word's effect at level 1, in any school except Passion: a bottle of FROST, GLUE or CALM. Anyone can use a potion as a bonus action. **Drink it**, and its effect works on the drinker and lasts for 10 minutes instead of its usual duration. **Throw it** up to 30 feet, and it bursts where it lands, and every creature within 5 feet gets the potion's full effect. Your potions spoil after your next long rest.
- **Level 5: Volatile Mixtures.** As a bonus action, you can combine two of your potions and drink or throw them together. They take effect as a single level 2 effect that mixes both words, such as FIRE and GLUE making sticky, burning tar. If the DM decides the words clash, roll a d6: on a 1 or 2, the mixture explodes in your hands for 1d6 damage per level the mixture would have had.
- **Level 10: Master Brewer.** Your potions hold level 2 effects, and your mixtures level 3. You can mix up to three potions at once, and thrown potions burst across 15 feet instead of 5. Once per long rest, one potion you brew can hold its effect at your Change rank.

\column

#### Runecarver
Runecarvers write on the things they carry into battle. Their blades and armor are covered in words, ready to be spoken with every swing.

- **Level 2: Carved Arms.** You can wear medium armor and use martial weapons. Your weapon and your armor can each hold one rune that doesn't count toward your limit.
- **Level 5: Bound Runes.** When a rune on your weapon or armor is used, it recharges after a short rest instead of fading.
- **Level 10: Words of Steel.** Once per combat, a rune on your weapon or armor takes effect 2 levels higher, up to your rank.

## Golemwright
Golemwrights are tinkers and inventors who build their magic instead of speaking it. They forge blades in the middle of a fight, patch broken bridges, and never travel alone: a construct of scrap and stubbornness walks at their side, growing stronger with every repair.

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light and simple weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 2 ranks to Create and 1 to Change.

**Handy.** Your mundane Change and Create actions, such as picking a lock or forging a blade, have a mortal limit of 4.

### Construct
During a long rest, you can build a construct companion out of scrap: wood, metal, stone, bone, or anything else sturdy, about as much as you could carry. You decide what it looks like, but it's never bigger than a person (Medium size in *Dungeons & Dragons*). You can only have one construct at a time.

- **Hit points and armor.** It has 10 hit points plus 3 per level you have, and 1 armor. It recovers hit points during rests like you do, and Aid spells and first aid heal it like a creature.
- **Actions.** It acts on your casting turn, right after you. It can move as far as you can, and make a weapon attack with the casting pool and weapon die shown in the Golemwright table. It can't cast spells or take other mundane actions, unless a feature says otherwise. Like you, it can carry objects and hand them to someone next to it as its action.
- **Commands.** It follows the commands you speak to it, which doesn't take an action. Without a command, it stays next to you and defends you.
- **Destroyed.** At 0 hit points, it's destroyed. You can build a new one during your next long rest.

\column

### Level 1: Modules
When you build your construct, choose a number of modules for it shown in the Golemwright table. You can swap them during a long rest. Each module can only be chosen once. The Spare Parts feat lets your construct have one more.

- **Plating.** It has 1 more armor.
- **Toolkit.** Instead of attacking, it can take a mundane Change or Create action, using its own casting pool.
- **Springs.** It moves twice as far as you can, and climbs and leaps as easily as it walks.
- **Ballista.** Its weapon attack can hit targets up to 60 feet away.
- **Lantern Eyes.** It can see in the dark, and you can have it shed bright light up to 30 feet around it.
- **Compartment.** A hidden compartment holds up to 3 objects that fit inside it, such as weapons, potions or runes. Nobody can see or take them while it's standing. Once per round, it can hand one to someone next to it, or launch it to an ally within 30 feet, without using its action.
- **Grapple Arm.** When its weapon attack hits, it can grab the target instead of dealing damage. The target is held like a Move effect of the attack's level, and tries to break free at the end of each of its turns.
- **Ram.** If it moves at least 15 feet in a straight line before its weapon attack hits, the target is also knocked down.
- **Smoke Vents.** Once per combat, as its action, it fills the area within 15 feet of it with thick smoke until the end of the next round. Creatures in the smoke can't see out of it, and can't be seen from outside it.
- **Sealed Hull.** It doesn't need air, works underwater, and floats. It can carry you across water like a small boat.
- **Whiskers.** It feels out the space around it. Hidden things within 10 feet of it have a concealment level 1 lower against your passive Reveal.

### Level 1: Tinker's Eye
Things you make with Create are one quality step better than their spell level, though not any bigger.

### Level 3: Field Repairs
Your mundane Change and Create actions can repair your construct, healing it like first aid but with d8s instead of d4s. If your construct is destroyed, you can rebuild it during a short rest instead of a long rest, with half its maximum hit points.

### Level 6: Upgraded Frame
Your construct now attacks with a casting pool of rank 3 and a d8 weapon die, and can have a second module.

### Level 9: Masterwork
Once per long rest, something you make with Create or a mundane Create action gains one lasting property from a school you have at rank 3 or higher. You can have 3 such items at once.

These properties come from clever engineering, not magic. A blade that burns for an extra 1d6 damage has an oil reservoir in its hilt that coats it in flame, and a cloak that keeps you warm is lined with a tiny clockwork stove. Because they aren't magic, nothing that stops or suppresses magic affects them.

### Level 12: Grand Engine
Your construct now attacks with a casting pool of rank 4 and a d10 weapon die, and can have a third module. Once per long rest, as your action, you can build a second construct from whatever is around you. It's a copy of your first, modules included, and falls apart after an hour.

\page

### Golemwright Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Improvised Tools.** Your mundane Change and Create actions don't need proper tools, and take half the usual time.
- **Guardian Protocol.** Once per round, your construct can take a hit for you or an ally, as long as you or they are next to it. Its armor applies.
- **Reinforced Construct (4+).** Your construct has 2 more hit points per level, and 2 armor.
- **Spare Parts (4+).** Your construct can have one more module.
- **Overdrive (8+).** Once per combat, during your pre-turn, you can push your construct past its limits. It acts twice on your casting turn this round.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Warforger
Warforgers build one construct, and build it big. Their companions are walking siege engines.

- **Level 2: Heavy Frame.** Your construct can be as big as a horse (Large size in *Dungeons & Dragons*), and has 1 more armor. You can ride it. While you do, you move with it instead of on your own, it moves twice as far as you can, and you add one die to your saving throws against being pushed, grabbed or knocked down.
- **Level 5: Sweeping Blows.** Once per round, your construct's weapon attack can hit the target and one other creature next to it, rolling damage separately.
- **Level 10: Colossus.** Once per long rest, your construct can grow huge for 3 rounds. It has twice its hit points, and its weapon die is one size bigger. Each time it attacks, choose: it hits a single target and rolls its weapon die twice for every level, or it stomps the ground and hits every enemy within 10 feet of it, rolling damage separately for each.

#### Clockmaker
Clockmakers prefer many small hands to one big one. Their workshops buzz with tiny brass helpers.

- **Level 2: Little Helpers.** Instead of one construct, you build a number of small constructs up to your Create rank, each no bigger than a cat. Divide your construct's hit points between them evenly, rounding down. They all have its armor and modules, and are destroyed and rebuilt one at a time. On your casting turn, one of them makes your construct's attack. Each of the others can do a simple task that needs no roll instead, such as fetching a key, holding a door open, or tugging at a guard's cloak.
- **Level 5: Swarm Tactics.** All your small constructs can attack on your casting turn, with a casting pool 1 rank lower than your construct's, to a minimum of rank 1.
- **Level 10: Brass Swarm.** Once per long rest, as your action, you release a swarm of tiny constructs that fills a 20-foot area for 3 rounds. At the end of each round, every enemy inside takes 3d6 damage, and every ally inside heals 1d6.

\column

#### Puppeteer
Puppeteers see through their constructs' eyes and speak through their mouths. Their constructs are an extension of themselves.

- **Level 2: Puppet Strings.** While your construct is within a mile of you, you can see and hear through it, and speak through it. Spells you speak through it are cast from where it stands.
- **Level 5: Conduit.** Once per combat, your construct can cast one of your saved spells on your casting turn, instead of attacking. Roll your pre-turn for it as normal.
- **Level 10: Living Vessel.** Once per long rest, you can move your mind into your construct for up to an hour. It uses your ranks, hit die and features, and your body falls unconscious. Your mind returns to your body if your construct is destroyed or your body takes any damage. If your construct is destroyed, you also take 2d8 damage.
