# Spellbound
### A Ruleset of Spoken Magic
</div>

## Introduction
Spellbound is a homebrew TTRPG ruleset that combines *Dungeons & Dragons* and *Spell: The RPG*. This ruleset is meant to allow for more creativity in casting spells by taking the spell creation mechanic from *Spell: The RPG* while adding general gameplay similar to *Dungeons & Dragons*.

Whenever a mechanic comes up that isn't explicitly stated in this ruleset, you should default to *Dungeons & Dragons* or make up a rule of your own. Spellbound relies heavily on improvising, both for the DM and the players, almost every round of combat. These rules will help, but do not hesitate to come up with fun ways to play your sessions yourself!

## What You Need
}}
Besides what you normally need to play *Dungeons & Dragons*, playing Spellbound in person also requires *Scrabble tiles*. It is recommended to have as many as you can, especially with higher level players, to avoid running out of tiles or having to share them, which could create longer waiting times during combat.

As an alternative to playing Spellbound physically, you can use the [Spellbound Dashboard](https://teddyara.github.io/spellbound/). This dashboard allows you to roll dice to cast spells and create and move letter tiles. There is also a mobile layout that works similar to *D&D Beyond*. It also allows you to roll dice and use letter tiles, but can also be used together with real dice and letter tiles.

## What Makes Spellbound Different
If you've played *Dungeons & Dragons*, most of Spellbound will feel familiar. A few things work very differently:

- **You make your own spells.** There's no spell list. You draw letter tiles and spell out whatever you want your magic to do, from STING to THE FLOOR TURNS TO ICE.
- **Schools describe goals, not methods.** A Harm spell can be a fireball, a falling boulder, or a swarm of bees. What matters is what the spell is meant to do.
- **Magic and skill use the same dice.** Every school has a mundane side, so the same Harm dice swing your sword, and the same Control dice talk your way past a guard.
- **Your dice grow with you.** Beginners roll a handful of d6s. Masters roll d8s, d10s and finally d12s, which can reach DCs no beginner ever could.
- **Your voice matters.** Spells are spoken out loud, so whispering, shouting, and who can hear you all change how a fight plays out.

## Magic in the World
In the world of Spellbound, magic is language. A caster reaches out with their mind and feels letters of power drifting around them, like sparks in the air. The stronger the caster, the more letters they can catch at once. The tiles you draw are the letters your character has managed to catch this round.

To cast a spell, a caster shapes those letters into words and speaks them aloud. A well-formed word turns into magic. A misspelled one still works, but the magic twists and lashes back at its caster, which is why bending the rules causes a backfire.

Letters a caster doesn't use slip away when their turn ends. Skilled casters can hold on to a few, whisper them to an ally, or even snatch them away from another caster before they're spoken.

## How This Book Is Organized
Spellbound is split into four parts. You don't need to read it front to back: start with the part that fits you, and come back to the rest when you need it.

### Playing the Game

- **Turn Structure.** How a round works, what you can do in your pre-turn and casting turn, and what happens outside of combat.
- **Schools of Magic.** The eight schools, what each one is for, and the mundane actions that go with them.
- **Rolling the Dice.** Casting pools, surges, difficulty and saving throws.
- **Spellcasting.** Spelling your spells, speaking them aloud, Tongues, and how strong a spell can get.
- **Health and Resting.** Hit points, armor, wards, resting, and what happens when you drop to 0.
- **Example of Play.** One full combat, from initiative to the last blow.

### Making a Character
- **Character Creation.** Eight steps from a blank sheet to a ready character.
- **The class chapters.** One for each of the seven classes, with their features, feats, subclasses and a Quick Build to start playing right away.
- **Feats.** The general feats every class can choose from.
- **Advancement.** What changes when you level up.

### For the DM
- **Running Enemies.** Building enemies and encounters for any party level, with example stat blocks.
- **Judging Spells.** Deciding quickly and fairly whether a spell works, and what happens when it backfires.

### Appendices
- **A: Quick Reference.** The tables you'll look up most at the table.
- **B: Glossary.** Every rules term in one place.
- **C: Example Spells.** Ideas for every school, for when you're stuck for words.
- **D: Character Sheet.** Copy or print one for each character.
- **E: Feature Interactions.** For the DM: which features change how others work.
- **F: Changes.** What's new in each version.

### Where to Start
- **New to tabletop games?** Read *Playing the Game*, then make a character with a Quick Build. Your DM can explain the rest as you go.
- **Played *Dungeons & Dragons* before?** Read *What Makes Spellbound Different*, then *Turn Structure*, *Rolling the Dice* and *Spellcasting*. Most of the rest will feel familiar.
- **Running the game?** Read what the players read, then *For the DM* and *Appendix E*.

### Reading the Rules
- **Spells are written in capitals,** like STING or THE FLOOR TURNS TO ICE.
- **Bold words** are rules terms. They're explained where they first appear, and again in the *Glossary*.
- ***Italic names*** point to another section of the book, like *Volume* or *Lasting Effects*.
- **"You"** means your character, and **"the DM"** means whoever runs the game.
- **Dice** are written as how many you roll and their size: 3d6 means three six-sided dice.
- **Specific beats general.** When a feature or feat contradicts a general rule, the feature or feat wins.

## Turn Structure
Before combat starts, everyone rolls for initiative to decide the order of turns. Roll a d20 and add your Move rank. Whoever has the highest total goes first, then the next highest, and so on. If two creatures tie, the one with the higher Reveal rank goes first. If they're still tied, players go before enemies.

Initiative stays the same for the whole combat.

### Rounds
During combat, every round is split into two parts: a **pre-turn** and a **casting turn**. Everyone takes their pre-turn, in initiative order. Once the last person or enemy's pre-turn has ended, it loops back around to the first person or enemy's casting turn.

### Pre-Turn
During your pre-turn, you get to choose from a list of actions:
1. **Cast a spell.** When you take this action, you're choosing to either create a new spell or cast a spell from your saved spell slots.
2. **Perform a mundane action.** This covers most actions, replacing actions from *Dungeons & Dragons* such as attacking and making ability checks.
3. **Prepare.** When you take this action, you're forgoing your casting turn to be able to cast a counter-spell against any spell you can hear until your next turn. After casting the counter-spell, regardless of whether it was successful, your Prepare action has run out.

Besides these, you can choose to use any other actions as described in *Dungeons & Dragons*. These actions are almost always performed during the casting turn rather than the pre-turn, so you simply announce them during your pre-turn.

#### Rolling Your Pre-Turn
Casting a spell and performing a mundane action both start with a roll. Tell the DM which school of magic your action falls under, and the DM gives you a difficulty (DC) in return. Roll your casting pool for that school (see *Rolling the Dice*):

- If at least one die equals or beats the DC, your action succeeds.
- Count every die that rolled a 5 or higher. This is your action's **level**. If none did, the level is 1. The level can never be higher than your rank in that school.
- If you're creating a new spell, draw letter tiles equal to the total of your roll. Mundane actions and saved spells draw no tiles.

\column

If your roll fails, you draw nothing and your chosen action does nothing this round. You still get your casting turn for movement and any other actions.

Your pre-turn is short on purpose. Use the time while others take their pre-turns to work out your spell from your tiles.

#### Prepare
If you take the Prepare action, you don't roll during your pre-turn. Instead, when a creature you can hear reveals its spell, you may counter it. Roll your casting pool for the school of the spell you're countering. Each surge lowers that spell's level by 1. If the level drops below 1, the spell fizzles entirely.

You can only counter once per Prepare action. Preparing uses up your casting turn's action, but you can still move.

### Casting Turn
Once every pre-turn has been taken, casting turns begin, in the same initiative order. On your casting turn, you resolve whatever you chose in your pre-turn:

- **New spell.** Spell out your spell with your tiles and explain how it fits the school of magic. If the DM approves, the spell takes effect at the level you rolled.
- **Bending the rules.** You may misspell a word or bend grammar to make your spell work. The DM can still approve it, but expect a backfire. That could be damage, a complication, or something worse.
- **No spell.** If you can't come up with a spell, keep a number of tiles equal to your spell's level. You can use them on your next casting turn.
- **Saved spell.** If your pre-turn roll succeeded, the spell takes effect at its saved level. No spelling needed.
- **Mundane action.** Describe what you do. It takes effect at the level you rolled, up to the mortal limit (see *Mundane Actions*).

On your casting turn, you can also move and take any other actions *Dungeons & Dragons* allows, such as interacting with an object. Reactions work as normal and can be taken at any time.

At the end of your casting turn, return all used tiles and any tiles you didn't keep to the pile.

#### Bonus Actions
Some features and items let you do something as a **bonus action**: a quick extra thing alongside your main action, such as drinking a potion or starting a Spellfury. You get one bonus action per round, and only if something lets you take one. Most bonus actions happen on your casting turn, but some, like starting a signature ability, happen during your pre-turn. The feature tells you which. A bonus action doesn't need a pre-turn roll unless it says otherwise.

\page

#### Opportunity Attacks
When an enemy you can see moves away from you, out of the space next to you, you can use your reaction to make a weapon attack against it. Roll your Harm casting pool right away, and if you succeed, the attack takes effect at the level you rolled. Enemies can do the same to you.

### Example Round
Mira, Bram and a goblin are in combat, in that initiative order.

**Pre-turns.** Mira wants to attack the goblin with magic. She chooses Harm, gets a DC of 4, and rolls her 3d6: 2, 4 and 5. Two dice meet the DC, so she succeeds. One die rolled a 5 or higher, so her spell is level 1, and she draws 11 tiles. Bram takes the Prepare action. The goblin chooses a mundane action to attack Mira with its spear and succeeds.

**Casting turns.** Mira spells STING and deals 1d8 damage to the goblin. Bram has nothing to counter yet, so he walks to Mira's side. The goblin stabs Mira. Since its attack isn't a spell, Bram's Prepare can't counter it, and his Prepare stays active until his next turn.

### Outside of Combat
Outside of combat, there are no pre-turns or casting turns. When you want to do something, tell the DM what you're trying and which school it falls under, then roll, spell, and resolve it in one go. Take as long as you need to spell, unless the DM says time is short.

### Distances
Distances are measured in feet, as in *Dungeons & Dragons*. When a rule says a creature is **next to you**, it means within 5 feet of you.

## Schools of Magic
There are eight schools of magic. Rather than being split up by what kind of spells you'd perform in each school, each school describes a spell's purpose or goal. They are:
1. **Harm.** Any magic meant to damage, destroy, or weaken.
2. **Aid.** Any magic meant to protect, restore, or support.
3. **Change.** Any magic meant to alter the properties or nature of something.
4. **Move.** Any magic meant to move something.
5. **Control.** Any magic meant to direct the behavior of creatures or objects.
6. **Reveal.** Any magic meant to gather or enhance information.
7. **Create.** The permanent creation of an object, creature, or phenomenon, with no purpose of its own.
8. **Passion.** Any magic that ties into your chosen passion.

Each school of magic can also be used for mundane actions, which are actions that are non-magical. Examples include melee attacks with Harm, athletics rolls with Move, and persuasion with Control.

### Harm
Harm spells deal damage, break objects, or weaken a target. A Harm spell deals 1d8 damage per spell level. You can share these dice between several creatures, giving each at least one die, so a Harm spell can hit as many creatures as its level (see *Sharing Effect Dice*).

**Spell examples.** Shooting a fireball, exploding a wall, cursing a knight's armor to rust.

**Mundane examples.** Swinging a sword, shooting an arrow, throwing a punch.

### Aid
Aid spells heal, shield, or support creatures and objects. An Aid spell that heals restores 1d8 hit points per spell level, and you can share these dice between several creatures, giving each at least one die. An Aid spell that creates a ward absorbs 1d8 damage per spell level until your next turn. Like healing, you can share these dice between several creatures or objects, giving each its own ward of at least one die. Any other Aid spell effects are up to the discretion of the DM.

**Spell examples.** A shield around an ally, mending a broken wall, giving a tired friend a second wind.

**Mundane examples.** Bandaging a wound, treating poison, guarding an ally.

\page

### Change
Change spells alter what something is or how it behaves. The bigger the change, the higher the level it needs: turning a lock is easy, turning a castle to glass is not.

**Spell examples.** Unlocking a lock, turning water into ice, making a rope as hard as steel.

**Mundane examples.** Picking a lock, putting on a disguise, mixing a potion.

### Move
Move spells move creatures or objects. The heavier the thing or the further it goes, the higher the level it needs.

**Spell examples.** A gust of wind knocking someone over, summoning a giant to lift a boulder, pulling a key across the room.

**Mundane examples.** Climbing, jumping, shoving, throwing, sneaking and hiding.

### Control
Control spells direct how creatures or objects behave. A creature affected against its will is controlled for a number of rounds equal to the spell's level. At the end of each of its turns, it may try to break free.

**Spell examples.** Making a guard let you through, vines wrapping around someone, stopping a set of gears.

**Mundane examples.** Persuading, deceiving, or intimidating someone.

### Reveal
Reveal spells give you information or sharpen your senses. A Reveal spell gives you one clear fact or detail per spell level, or a sense that lasts 10 minutes per spell level.

**Spell examples.** Night-vision, detecting magic, hearing what was said in a room an hour ago.

**Mundane examples.** Searching a room, tracking footprints, reading someone's intentions.

#### Passive Reveal
You don't have to roll to notice everything. Your Reveal rank is also your **passive Reveal**. It replaces Passive Perception from *Dungeons & Dragons*.

When something is hidden, the DM gives it a concealment level from 1 to 8. If your Reveal rank equals or beats it, you notice it automatically. If it doesn't, you can still find it by searching, which means rolling for a Reveal spell or mundane action. Something hidden by a spell has a concealment level equal to that spell's level.

\column

| <div style="width:40px">Rank</div> | You notice without trying |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | What's obvious: an open door, a loud noise, someone standing in plain view |
| 2 | What's easy to miss: footprints in mud, a whiff of smoke, a clumsy pickpocket |
| 3 | What's carelessly hidden: a poorly hidden lever, an ambush in tall grass, a nervous liar |
| 4 | What's hidden with care: a trapdoor under a rug, a trained scout, a forged signature |
| 5 | What's expertly hidden: a secret door, a professional assassin, poison in a drink |
| 6 | What few mortals would notice: a disguised shapeshifter, faint traces of magic |
| 7 | What's masterfully hidden: an invisible creature's footsteps, a flaw in an archmage's illusion |
| 8 | What's nearly impossible to see: a god in disguise, a lie told by someone who believes it |

### Create
Create spells make something that stays indefinitely, unless something special makes it disappear. Unlike every other school, a Create spell has no purpose: what you create won't do anything on your behalf, and might have a will of its own. A skeleton summoned with Harm attacks your target and disappears afterwards. The same skeleton made with Create stays, but might attack you, attack your enemy, or simply run away.

The spell's level decides how good your creation is. A level 1 sword is chipped and dull; a level 6 sword rivals the finest blades in the land.

**Spell examples.** A sword, a ray of light, a wooden bridge.

**Mundane examples.** Building a shelter, forging a blade, cooking a meal.

### Passion
When you create your character, you choose a passion. This can be anything, such as the undead, the sea, or music. Any spell that ties into your passion can be cast with Passion. If your passion is the undead and you spell PULL DOWN, skeleton arms might rise from the ground to drag your target down.

Because Passion is so limited, it's also strong. Your Passion rank is always 2 higher than your highest rank in any other school, up to a maximum of 8. In return, your DM will be stricter about whether a Passion spell works, and may ask you to justify it. Why would there be skeletons under your target?

Try not to choose a passion that's too specific, or you'll struggle to use it.

**Mundane examples.** Knowledge and skills related to your passion, such as identifying an old bone or calming a spooked horse.

\page

### Mundane Actions
Mundane actions work like spells, with a few differences:
- **No spelling.** You roll your casting pool for the school as normal, but you don't draw tiles. On your casting turn, you just describe what you do.
- **Mortal limit.** A mundane action can never be higher than level 3, no matter what you roll. Some class features and feats raise this limit.
- **Plausibility.** A mundane action has to be something a skilled person could do with the tools they have (see *Tools*). You can't punch a hole through a castle wall, however well you roll.
- **No backfire.** Mundane actions never backfire.
- **Time.** Most mundane actions take a single casting turn. Making or fixing things with Change or Create takes longer: about a minute for something small and simple, like picking a lock or patching a cloak, an hour for something you can carry, like forging a dagger, and a day or more for anything bigger. The DM has the final say.

#### Mundane Action Levels
A mundane action's level decides how well it goes. Damage and healing use effect dice, like spells (see *Effect Dice*).

| <div style="width:40px">Level</div> | Result | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | A basic success | Climbing a rough wall, a glancing blow |
| 2 | A clean success | Picking a sturdy lock, calming a wary guard |
| 3 | An excellent result, the best most people manage | Leaping a wide chasm, a convincing forgery |
| 4 | Expert work, only with a feature | Talking a noble into betraying their lord |
| 5 | Masterful, the peak of mortal skill | Scaling a sheer cliff in a storm |

\column

## Rolling the Dice
Almost everything you do in Spellbound starts with the same roll: your **casting pool**. Whether you're casting a fireball or swinging a sword, you roll a handful of dice for the school of magic your action falls under. That one roll tells you three things: whether you succeed, how powerful the action is, and how many letter tiles you get.

### Ranks
Every character has a rank in each school of magic, from 1 to 8. Your rank shows how skilled you are in that school, both with magic and with its mundane actions. You set your starting ranks when you create your character, and raise them as you level up (see *Advancement*).

Your Passion rank works differently: it's always 2 higher than your highest rank in any other school, up to a maximum of 8.

### Casting Pools
Your rank decides which dice you roll. At low ranks you roll more d6s as you improve. From rank 5 onward, your dice get bigger instead.

##### Casting Pools

| Rank | Casting pool |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | 1d6 |
| 2 | 2d6 |
| 3 | 3d6 |
| 4 | 4d6 |
| 5 | 4d8 |
| 6 | 5d8 |
| 7 | 5d10 |
| 8 | 5d12 |

Bigger dice matter for two reasons. They roll 5 or higher more often, which makes your actions stronger, and they can roll higher than 6, which lets you attempt feats that are impossible for less skilled characters.

### Reading Your Roll
When you roll your casting pool, look at it three ways:

1. **Did you succeed?** If at least one die equals or beats the DC, your action succeeds. If none do, it fails.
2. **How strong is it?** Every die that rolled a 5 or higher is called a **surge**. The number of surges is your action's **level**. If you rolled no surges, the level is 1. Your level can never be higher than your rank in that school.
3. **How many tiles do you get?** If you're creating a new spell, add up all your dice and draw that many letter tiles. There's no limit. Mundane actions and saved spells don't draw tiles.

A failed roll gives you nothing: no tiles, no level, and no action this round.

\page

### Difficulty
Your DM sets the difficulty (DC) based on what you're trying to do and who or what you're doing it to.

| <div style="width:40px">DC</div> | <div style="width:60px">Difficulty</div> | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 2 | Trivial | Lighting a candle, opening an unlocked door |
| 3 | Easy | Climbing a rope, calming a dog |
| 4 | Standard | Hitting an ordinary guard, picking a simple lock |
| 5 | Hard | Hitting a trained knight, breaking a warded door |
| 6 | Very hard | Fighting an elite soldier, opening a vault |
| 7–8 | Heroic | Piercing a dragon's hide, breaking a royal ward |
| 9–10 | Legendary | Outwitting an archmage, binding a god's servant |
| 11–12 | Mythic | Facing a demigod, bending the laws of a realm |

Some dice can never roll high enough to succeed at the hardest DCs. A d6 can never roll higher than 6, so a DC of 7 or higher needs at least rank 5 (d8), a DC of 9 or higher needs at least rank 7 (d10), and a DC of 11 or 12 needs rank 8 (d12).

The DM can raise the DC by 1 or 2 if your spell or action is a poor fit for the target, and lower it if you're exploiting a weakness. If you think a DC is unfair, you can always plead your case.

#### Player Guard DC
When an enemy aims a spell or mundane action at a player character, the DC is that character's **Guard DC**: 4 at levels 1–4, 5 at levels 5–8, and 6 at levels 9–12. Like an enemy's Guard DC, the DM can raise it by 1 or 2 when the attack suits the target poorly, such as an arrow at someone behind cover, and lower it when the attack exploits a weakness, such as a target that's distracted. A Golemwright's construct has the same Guard DC as the Golemwright.

### Adding and Losing Dice
Some features, feats and situations add dice to your casting pool or take them away. An added die is always the same size as the rest of your pool: a rank 5 character who gains a die rolls 5d8 instead of 4d8. Your pool can never drop below one die.

Gaining a die doesn't change your rank, so your level is still capped by your rank.

### Rerolls
Some features let you reroll dice. You always have to keep the new result, even if it's worse. You can't reroll a die more than once.

\column

### Saving Throws
Sometimes something happens to you, and you have to resist it: a trap springs, poison hits your blood, or an enemy's spell grabs hold of you. When that happens, the DM asks for a **saving throw**. Roll the casting pool of the school that fits how you resist. If at least one die equals or beats the DC, you resist.

| <div style="width:60px">School</div> | Resists | Instead of |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Move | Being pushed, grabbed or knocked down; dodging falling rocks, blasts and traps | Strength and Dexterity saves |
| Aid | Poison, disease, exhaustion, extreme heat or cold | Constitution saves |
| Reveal | Illusions, tricks, and things that aren't what they seem | Intelligence saves |
| Control | Charm, fear, and anything that tries to take over your mind | Wisdom and Charisma saves |

If you're using an adventure written for *Dungeons & Dragons*, use the last column to translate its saving throws.

**Against hazards,** the DM sets the DC like any other difficulty. **Against spells,** the DC is the spell's level + 3, so a level 2 spell needs a 5 or higher to resist. This means a spell of level 4 or higher can only be resisted by characters with bigger dice.

A successful saving throw usually means you avoid the effect entirely, or take half damage if it's something you can't fully escape, like an explosion. Damaging spells aimed straight at you don't allow a saving throw unless the DM says otherwise.

### Example
Ash has Move at rank 5 and wants to throw a bandit off a bridge. The DM sets the DC at 6. Ash rolls 4d8 and gets 1, 3, 5 and 8.

- **Success.** The 8 beats the DC of 6, so the action succeeds.
- **Level.** The 5 and 8 are surges, so the action is level 2.
- **Tiles.** Ash is casting a new spell, so they draw 1 + 3 + 5 + 8 = 17 tiles.

On their casting turn, Ash spells WIND GUST and blasts the bandit off the bridge.

## Spellcasting
In Spellbound, you don't pick spells from a list. You make them up by spelling them out with your letter tiles. What a spell does depends on three things: the school you rolled, the word(s) you spelled, and the level you rolled.

### Creating a Spell
On your casting turn, use your tiles to spell out your spell. Then tell the DM what the spell does and how it fits the school you rolled. If the DM approves, the spell takes effect.

A few rules for spelling:
- **Any real word counts.** Nouns, verbs, adjectives, names of places or creatures in your world: if it's a real word, you can use it.
- **As many words as you like.** A spell can be a single word like STING, a phrase like POISON LUNG, or a whole sentence like THE FLOOR TURNS TO ICE, as long as you have the tiles.
- **Blank tiles.** Blank tiles can be used as any letter.
- **No abbreviations or made-up words.** You can only use these when you're bending the rules (see *Bending the Rules*).

The word or words you spell shape the spell, but they don't decide how strong it is. Spelling BECOME GOD at level 1 won't make you a god, but you might get one small divine act. A short spell can be just as powerful as a long one if you roll well.

### Speaking Your Spell
Spells are cast out loud. When your spell takes effect, your character speaks its words, and anyone within hearing distance can hear them (see *Volume*).

- **You need your voice.** If you're gagged, magically silenced, or underwater, you can't cast spells, including saved spells. You can still take mundane actions. Those fluent in the Drowned Tongue can cast underwater (see *Tongues*).
- **Spells are heard.** Casting a spell gives away your position if you're hiding, and anyone who hears you knows a spell is coming. This is what lets others counter your spells with the Prepare action.
- **Volume matters.** How loudly you speak decides how far your spell reaches and who can hear it (see *Volume*).

Some class abilities and feats let you cast spells in other ways, such as the Runesmith's Written Word.

#### Volume
When you cast a spell, choose whether to whisper, speak, or shout it. If you don't say, you speak it. The louder you are, the further your spell reaches, but the more people hear it coming.

\column

| <div style="width:60px">Volume</div> | Spell range | Heard within |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Whisper | Touch: something next to you | 5 feet |
| Speak | 60 feet | 60 feet |
| Shout | 120 feet | 300 feet |

Every creature within hearing distance hears your spell. It can counter the spell if it took the Prepare action, and if you were hiding, it knows where you are.

A spell's range is measured in distance, not sound, so a wall between you and your target doesn't stop the spell from reaching it. Whether you can target something you can't see is up to your DM.

#### What Blocks Sound
Obstacles and noise make you harder to hear. Each one makes you one step quieter for anyone on the other side of it: a shout is heard as if you spoke, a spoken spell as if you whispered, and a whisper isn't heard at all.

- **One step quieter.** A closed door, a thin wall, a curtain of falling water, or a very loud place such as a storm or a crowded tavern.
- **Two steps quieter.** A thick stone wall, or being underwater.

These only change who hears your spell, not its range.

A creature that's deafened can't hear any spells, so it can't counter them.

### Tongues
The letters of power are the same everywhere, but every tradition shapes them with its own grammar, called a **Tongue**. Every spell is cast in a Tongue. If you know a spell's Tongue, you can follow its magic: read it on someone's lips, or copy it. If you don't, you only hear the words.

Tongues only matter for magic. Everyday talk uses ordinary languages, so you can still chat with anyone.

- **Which Tongue.** You cast in your native Tongue, unless you choose another Tongue you know when you reveal your spell. Written spells, including runes, have no Tongue: anyone can read them.
- **Knowing Tongues.** You start with two: your native Tongue, which you speak **fluently**, and one more that you speak well. You can learn more with the Polyglot feat, or with weeks of downtime and a teacher, if the DM allows. Learning a Tongue you already speak a second time makes you fluent in it.
- **What they affect.** Lip Reader, Silent Casting, Copycat, Perfect Mimicry, Perfect Heist and Duet depend on Tongues. Speaking a Tongue well is enough for these. Countering doesn't: you only need to hear a spell to counter it. Neither does anything that moves tiles, since letters are the same in every Tongue. Runes, potions and constructs work for anyone.

\page

| Tongue | Spoken by | How it sounds |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Hearthspeech | Common folk, most travellers | Warm and plain |
| Deepcant | Dwarves, underground creatures | Clipped and heavy |
| Leafweave | Elves, fey, druids | Soft and looping |
| Ashen | Dragons, giants, demons | Old and grand |
| Drowned Tongue | Sailors, merfolk, sea witches | Rolling and wet |
| Hollow Tongue | The undead, cultists, things from beyond | Deep and echoing |

#### Tongue Perks
Every Tongue you're fluent in gives you a small perk: your native Tongue, and any Tongue you've learned twice. Tongues you only speak well let you follow their magic, but don't give you their perk.

- **Hearthspeech: Small Comforts.** Outside of combat, you can do trivial magic without rolling or drawing tiles, like lighting a candle, warming a meal or cleaning a muddy cloak. It can't harm anyone, and it isn't a spell you can save.
- **Deepcant: Stone Echo.** Tap a stone surface and listen. You learn the shape of the tunnels, rooms and hollow spaces within 30 feet, even in total darkness. It doesn't reveal creatures.
- **Leafweave: Green Words.** You can share simple ideas with animals and plants, like "danger" or "which way did they go?" They understand you, but they don't have to obey.
- **Ashen: Old Authority.** Dragons, giants, demons and other ancient creatures that know Ashen will hear you out before they attack. A bargain can be sealed with Ashen magic, which binds both creatures: they won't break it openly, though they may twist its wording.
- **Drowned Tongue: Deep Voice.** You can speak underwater, and cast spoken spells there too. You still can't breathe there.
- **Hollow Tongue: Death's Ear.** When a creature speaks, you can tell how close it is to death: unhurt, wounded, badly hurt, or barely standing.

### Justifying Your Spell
The DM approves your spell if it makes sense for what you want to do, and what you want to do fits the school. Spelling STING with Harm to hurt a goblin is easy to approve. Spelling STING with Move to push a boulder will need a good explanation.

If the DM isn't convinced, you can plead your case. If they still say no, the spell doesn't work, but you may keep tiles as if you couldn't spell anything (see *When You Can't Spell*).

\column

### Spell Levels
The level you rolled decides how powerful your spell is. If you're unsure whether something is possible at a certain level, ask your DM or plead your case.

| <div style="width:40px">Level</div> | Scale | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Minor magic affecting one creature or object | Tripping a guard, lighting a candle |
| 2 | Several targets, or a stronger effect | Knocking three bandits over |
| 3 | A group, or a notable effect | Filling a courtyard with fog, healing a badly hurt ally |
| 4 | Large scale, with significant impact | Destroying a gate, healing your entire party |
| 5 | A room or a small building | Collapsing a tower floor, calming a riot |
| 6 | Great magic | Creating a huge explosion, moving a building |
| 7 | Legendary magic | Turning a river, holding back a small army |
| 8 | Mythic magic, rarely seen | Raising an island, ending a storm |

For damage and healing, the effect dice already decide how strong a spell is and how many creatures it can reach, so use *Effect Dice* instead of this table.

### Effect Dice
When a spell or mundane action needs a number, such as damage or healing, roll a number of dice equal to its level.

| Action | Effect dice | Several targets |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Harm spell | 1d8 damage per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Aid spell (heal) | Heal 1d8 per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Aid spell (ward) | Absorbs 1d8 damage per level until your next turn | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Passion spell | 1d6 damage or healing per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Melee or ranged attack (Harm, mundane) | Your weapon die per level | One target only |
| First aid (Aid, mundane) | Heal 1d4 per level | One target only |

Some features make effect dice one size bigger. These add up: a d6 made two sizes bigger becomes a d10. Effect dice and weapon dice can never grow beyond a d12.

\page

#### Sharing Effect Dice
A spell that damages, heals or creates a ward can affect several creatures by sharing its effect dice between them. Every creature gets at least one die, so a spell can affect as many creatures as its level. Roll each creature's dice separately.

A level 3 Harm spell rolls 3d8. It can hit one goblin for 3d8, two goblins for 2d8 and 1d8, or three goblins for 1d8 each. In the same way, a level 3 ward can be one strong 3d8 ward, or a 1d8 ward on each of three allies.

### Lasting Effects
Some spells don't just happen and end, like a Control spell that holds someone in place.

- **Against unwilling creatures.** Change, Move and Control spells on a creature that doesn't want them last a number of rounds equal to the spell's level. At the end of each of its turns, the creature may try to break free. Player characters make a saving throw against the spell (see *Saving Throws*). Enemies roll their **resist die**, a d6, d8, d10 or d12 depending on how powerful they are, and break free if they roll higher than the spell's level + 2.
- **Outside of combat.** These effects last for the rest of the scene.
- **Reveal spells.** These give you one clear fact or detail per level, or a sense that lasts 10 minutes per level.
- **Create spells.** These are permanent.
- **Anything else.** These effects last until the end of your next turn, unless the DM decides otherwise.

### Creation Quality
Things you make with Create get better and bigger as the spell's level goes up.

| <div style="width:40px">Level</div> | Quality | Largest size |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Crude: a chipped, dull sword | Something you can hold |
| 2 | Serviceable | Something you can carry |
| 3 | Good, well made | Furniture |
| 4 | Fine | A cart |
| 5 | Masterwork | A house |
| 6 | Exceptional | A hall |
| 7 | Legendary | A fortress |
| 8 | Wondrous | A castle |

### Bending the Rules
Sometimes the tiles just won't cooperate. You can misspell a word or bend grammar to make your spell work, such as spelling ISE SPIKE instead of ICE SPIKE. If the DM approves, the spell works as normal, but it backfires.

The DM decides what the backfire is. It's usually related to how you bent the rules: misspelling ICE might give you frostbite. If the DM can't think of anything, you take 1d4 damage per spell level.

\column

### When You Can't Spell
If you can't come up with a spell, you can keep a number of tiles equal to the level you rolled. Choose which ones. You can use them on your next casting turn, together with any new tiles you draw.

### Saving Spells
If you really liked a spell, you can save it. After the spell succeeds, use one of your empty spell slots. From then on, you know that spell at level 1. Most characters start with 2 spell slots and gain more as they level up (see *Advancement*).

To cast a saved spell, choose it during your pre-turn and roll your casting pool for its school as normal. You don't draw tiles. If your roll succeeds, the spell takes effect on your casting turn at its saved level.

Saved spells get stronger the more you use them. Every time you cast one successfully, roll one die of the same size as your casting pool for that school. If the result equals or beats the spell's current level, the spell's level goes up by 1. A saved spell can never be higher than your rank in its school.

During a long rest, you can forget one saved spell to free up its spell slot.

### Example
Rowan has Harm at rank 3. During her pre-turn, she attacks a group of three cultists. The DM sets the DC at 4, and Rowan rolls 3d6: 2, 5 and 6. She succeeds, rolls two surges, and draws 13 tiles.

On her casting turn, she wants to hit as many cultists as she can. She doesn't have the letters for FIREBALL, but she can spell EMBER RAIN. The DM approves. The spell is level 2, so it rolls 2d8, which she can share between up to two creatures: she gives one die to each of two cultists, and each takes 1d8 damage.

Rowan likes the spell, so she uses a spell slot to save EMBER RAIN at level 1. The next time she casts it successfully, she rolls a d6 to see if it grows. She rolls a 3, which beats its level of 1, so EMBER RAIN is now a level 2 saved spell.

{{note
##### Spell Creation
These rules depend heavily on the type of campaign you're playing. More serious campaigns could require spells to fit the theme of the world, not allowing spells like AIOLI MAZE despite it being spelled correctly, while more laid-back campaigns could allow spells incorporating memes or inside jokes.
}}

## Health and Resting
Adventuring is dangerous. This chapter covers how your character gets hurt, how they recover, and what happens when things go badly wrong.

### Hit Points
Your **hit points** show how much punishment you can take before you fall. Your maximum hit points come from your class (see *Character Creation* and *Advancement*). When you take damage, subtract it from your current hit points. When you're healed, add it back, up to your maximum.

### Armor and Shields
Armor reduces every instance of damage you take by its armor value, to a minimum of 1 damage. A goblin's spear that deals 5 damage only deals 3 to someone in medium armor.

A **shield** adds 1 to your armor value, but it takes up one hand. Only classes and feats that say so let you use a shield. You can't hold a two-handed weapon while using one, and you'll need your other hand free for things like a Runesmith's Written Word.

### Wards
A **ward** is anything that stands between you and harm and absorbs damage for you: a shimmering barrier, a guardian spirit, or a Warden's raised shield. Wards come from Aid spells and from some features. Damage hits the ward first, and anything left over goes through to you. A ward lasts until its creator's next turn, unless the feature that made it says otherwise, or until it has absorbed as much damage as it can. If you have more than one ward, only the one that can still absorb the most damage counts.

### Resting
Adventurers need to rest to recover.

- **Short rest.** At least an hour of downtime: eating, bandaging wounds, catching your breath. You can take up to two short rests a day. Every short rest, roll a number of your class's hit dice equal to half your level, rounded up, and heal that many hit points.
- **Long rest.** At least 8 hours, most of it sleeping. You can take one long rest a day. At the end of a long rest, you regain all your hit points, any uses of your signature ability, and every feature that works "once per long rest".

### Once Per…
Many features can only be used a limited number of times.

- **Once per round** resets at the start of every round of combat.
- **Once per combat** resets when a combat ends.
- **Once per long rest** resets when you finish a long rest.

### Dropping to 0 Hit Points
When you drop to 0 hit points, you're **downed**. You fall unconscious, you can't take pre-turns or casting turns, and you can't speak, so you can't cast spells.

At the start of every round while you're downed, make a **death roll**: roll a d20.

- **10 or higher.** You gain a success.
- **9 or lower.** You gain a failure.
- **A natural 1.** You gain two failures.
- **A natural 20.** You wake up with 1 hit point.

Taking damage while you're downed also gives you a failure. When you have three successes, you're stable: you stay downed, but stop rolling, and wake up with 1 hit point after an hour. When you have three failures, your character dies.

Any healing, from an Aid spell, first aid or a potion, wakes you up immediately with that many hit points and clears all your successes and failures. An ally can also **stabilize** you with a successful mundane Aid action, just as if you'd rolled three successes.

{{note
##### Lasting Scars
Not every table wants characters to die. If your group prefers, a character with three failures survives instead, but gains a lasting scar chosen by the DM: a limp, a missing finger, a voice that no longer carries when they shout. This fits Spellbound especially well when the scar affects how they cast.
}}

### Conditions
Some effects leave you in a particular state. These are the conditions Spellbound uses most. For any others, such as poisoned or frightened, use the rules from *Dungeons & Dragons*.

- **Deafened.** You can't hear. You can't counter spells you can't hear, and you can't benefit from songs like a Loresinger's Battle Hymn.
- **Silenced.** You can't make a sound. You can't cast spoken spells, but you can still take mundane actions, and write spells if you're able to.
- **Hidden.** No one knows exactly where you are. Casting a spell out loud gives away your position to everyone who hears it (see *Volume*).
- **Distracted.** Your attention is elsewhere, usually because you're fighting someone else. A Trickster's Ambush works against distracted creatures.
- **Downed.** You're at 0 hit points and unconscious (see *Dropping to 0 Hit Points*).

## Example of Play
This chapter follows one full combat from start to finish, so you can see how the rules fit together. The party is three level 1 characters: Tamsin the Wordweaver from *Character Creation*, and Pip the Trickster and Bram the Warden, built with their classes' Quick Builds.

The party opens a crypt door and finds a cult acolyte standing right next to Bram, with two goblin skirmishers behind it. The party level is 1, so the goblins have 5 hit points and a Guard DC of 3, and the acolyte has 13 hit points, 1 armor and a Guard DC of 4.

### Initiative
Everyone rolls a d20 and adds their Move rank. The two goblins share a roll.

| Creature | Roll | Initiative |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Tamsin | 12 + 3 | 15 |
| Goblins | 11 + 2 | 13 |
| Pip | 9 + 3 | 12 |
| Bram | 7 + 2 | 9 |
| Acolyte | 5 + 1 | 6 |

### Round 1: Pre-Turns
- **Tamsin** wants to hit both goblins with a Harm spell. The DC is their Guard DC of 3. She rolls 4d6 and gets 1, 3, 5 and 6. Three dice meet the DC, so she succeeds. The 5 and 6 are surges, so her spell is level 2, and she draws 1 + 3 + 5 + 6 = 15 tiles.
- **The goblins** both shoot at Pip with their shortbows, a mundane Harm action. The DC is Pip's Guard DC of 4. The first goblin rolls 1 and 3 and fails. The second rolls 4 and 2, and succeeds at level 1.
- **Pip** attacks the acolyte with his rapier, a mundane Harm action against its Guard DC of 4. He rolls 2, 4, 5 and 1. He succeeds, and the 5 makes it level 1.
- **Bram** suspects the acolyte is about to cast something, so he takes the Prepare action instead of rolling.
- **The acolyte** casts its known spell OBEY at Bram, a Control spell. It rolls 4 and 2 against Bram's Guard DC of 4, and succeeds.

\column

### Round 1: Casting Turns
- **Tamsin** spells HAIL with her tiles and speaks it aloud. Her spell is level 2, so it rolls 2d8, and she gives one die to each goblin. The first goblin takes 7 and falls, before it ever gets its casting turn. The second takes 4 and is left with 1 hit point. With her bonus action, she uses her Cantrips feat to spell ARM, a level 1 Aid spell, and wraps Pip in a ward that absorbs 1d8 damage. She rolls a 3.
- **The first goblin** is down, so it does nothing. **The second goblin** shoots Pip for a d6, rolling 4. The ward absorbs 3 of it, and Pip's armor would take the last point away, but armor can never lower a hit below 1 damage. Pip takes 1 damage.
- **Pip** stabs the acolyte for 1d8, rolling 5. Bram is next to the acolyte, so Pip's Ambush adds 2d6, for another 7. That's 12 damage, minus the acolyte's 1 armor: 11. The acolyte is down to 2 hit points.
- **Bram** spent his casting turn preparing, so he waits.
- **The acolyte** points at Bram and speaks OBEY, commanding him to kneel. Bram counters: he rolls his casting pool for Control, the school of the spell, which is 3d6. He rolls 2, 5 and 6. Two surges lower the spell from level 1 to below 1, so it fizzles.

### Round 2
- **Pre-turns.** Tamsin aims a Harm spell at the wounded goblin and rolls 2, 2, 1 and 4. The 4 beats its DC of 3, so she succeeds at level 1 and draws 9 tiles. The goblin decides to run, and succeeds at a mundane Move action. Pip attacks the acolyte again, but rolls 3, 1, 2 and 2 against its Guard DC of 4, and fails. Bram rolls his Harm casting pool to swing his warhammer at the acolyte, getting 6, 3, 2 and 1: a success at level 1. The acolyte tries to CURSE Bram, but rolls 1 and 3, and fails.
- **Casting turns.** Tamsin goes first and spells BOLT. The goblin takes 1d8, rolling a 2, and falls before it can flee. Pip's attack failed, so he only steps around the acolyte to block its escape. When Bram's turn comes, his warhammer deals 1d8, rolling a 6. After the acolyte's armor, that's 5 damage, more than enough to drop it.

The fight is over in two rounds. Pip took 1 damage, thanks to Tamsin's quick thinking, and Bram's prepared counter stopped the acolyte's only successful spell.

## Character Creation
Creating a character in Spellbound is quicker than in *Dungeons & Dragons*. There are no ability scores to roll: everything your character can do comes from their ranks, their class, and their feats.

### 1. Choose a Class
Your class decides how tough you are, what you can wear and wield, and which features you gain as you level up. Every class can cast spells, but some lean more on magic and others on mundane actions. Each class also has a **signature ability** that only it has, and that defines how it plays, such as the Wordweaver's Torrent of Words, the Runesmith's Written Word or the Golemwright's Construct. Most signature abilities can be used a limited number of times, shown in the class's table.

| Class | Role |
|:--- |:--- |
| Wordweaver | Pure caster | 
| Loresinger | Support | 
| Runesmith | Trapper | 
| Golemwright | Crafter |
| Trickster | Skirmisher | 
| Spellblade | Warrior-mage | 
| Warden | Protector | 

### 2. Set Your Ranks
All seven schools (every school except Passion) start at rank 1. You then have 10 points to distribute among them as you like, adding 1 rank per point. After that, add your class's 3 bonus ranks.

No school other than Passion can be higher than rank 4 at level 1, including after your class bonus.

{{note
##### Spreading Your Points
A character with a few high ranks is powerful in their specialty, but struggles outside it. A character with many medium ranks can try anything, but rarely casts high-level spells. Remember that your highest rank also sets your Passion rank, Move sets your initiative, Reveal sets your passive Reveal, and Move, Aid, Reveal and Control cover your saving throws.
}}

### 3. Choose Your Passion
Pick something your character truly cares about, such as the sea, music, the undead, or clockwork. Your Passion rank is your highest other rank + 2, so most characters start with a Passion rank of 6. See *Passion* for advice on choosing one.

### 4. Choose a Feat
Pick one feat, either from the general feats or from your class's own list. See *Feats*.

\column

### 5. Determine Your Hit Points
At level 1, your maximum hit points are the highest number on your class's hit die + 6. A Warden starts with 18 hit points, while a Wordweaver starts with 12.

### 6. Choose Your Equipment
Choose one weapon and one type of armor that your class allows, plus ordinary adventuring gear like a backpack, rope, and rations, and any tools or inks you need. Your DM may let you start with more.

**Weapons.** When you attack with a weapon as a mundane action, you roll its weapon die once per level.

| Type | Weapon die | Examples | 
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Light | d4 | Dagger, sling | 
| Simple | d6 | Club, spear, shortbow | 
| Martial | d8 | Longsword, longbow | 
| Heavy | d10 | Greataxe, maul | 

**Armor.** Armor reduces every instance of damage you take by its armor value, to a minimum of 1 damage.

| Type | Armor value | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| None | 0 | Robes, everyday clothes |
| Light | 1 | Leather, padded cloth |
| Medium | 2 | Chain shirt, scale |
| Heavy | 3 | Chain mail, plate |
| Shield | +1 | Wooden shield, kite shield |

#### Tools
A mundane action has to be something a skilled person could do with the tools they have. These are the most common:

| Tools | Used for |
|:--- |:--- |
| Thieves' tools | Picking locks and disarming traps |
| Scribe's kit | Copying, forging and mending writing |
| Engraver's tools | Carving words and runes into wood, stone and metal |
| Tinker's tools | Building, fixing and taking apart small mechanisms |
| Smith's tools | Forging and repairing metal weapons, armor and objects. Bigger work needs a forge |
| Carpenter's tools | Building and repairing things made of wood |
| Alchemist's kit | Brewing potions, acids and powders |
| Herbalism kit | Finding herbs and making salves and remedies |
| Healer's kit | First aid |

\page

#### Inks
Anyone who writes spells or runes, such as a Runesmith, can carry special inks. What a spell or rune is written in decides what it survives.

| Ink | Property |
|:--- |:--- |
| Common ink | Runs in water and burns in fire |
| Waterproof ink | Survives rain, rivers and washing |
| Fireproof ink | Survives fire and great heat |
| Invisible ink | Can't be seen until it's heated, or found with a Reveal spell |
| Glowing ink | Gives dim light within 5 feet, and can be read in the dark |
| Floating ink | Can be drawn in the air, so spells and runes need no surface |

### 7. Note Your Spell Slots
You start with 2 empty spell slots (Wordweavers start with 3). You fill them by saving spells you've cast during play. See *Saving Spells*.

### 8. Fill in the Details
Finally, decide who your character is: their name, where they come from, what they look like, and what drives them.

You can choose a species and background from *Dungeons & Dragons* to help shape your character. Keep any traits that don't depend on ability scores, skills or languages, such as darkvision, and ignore ability score increases, skill proficiencies and languages, since Spellbound has none of them. A background is also a great way to explain your passion: a former sailor might well be passionate about the sea.

**Tongues.** Choose your native Tongue, which you speak fluently, and one more that you speak well (see *Tongues*). Your species and background are a good guide: a dwarf probably grew up with Deepcant, and a former sailor with the Drowned Tongue.

### Starting at a Higher Level
For a oneshot, your DM might ask you to start at level 3 so everyone has a few more features to play with. See *Advancement*.

\column

### Example
Tamsin is making a Wordweaver. She wants a character who's good at fighting, but can also solve problems in clever ways.

**Ranks.** All her schools start at rank 1. She spends her 10 points: 2 on Harm, 2 on Move, 2 on Reveal, and 1 each on Aid, Change, Control and Create. Her Wordweaver bonus adds 1 more to Harm, Change and Create.

| School | Rank | Casting pool |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Harm | 4 | 4d6 |
| Aid | 2 | 2d6 |
| Change | 3 | 3d6 |
| Move | 3 | 3d6 |
| Control | 2 | 2d6 |
| Reveal | 3 | 3d6 |
| Create | 3 | 3d6 |
| Passion (storms) | 6 | 5d8 |

**Passion.** Tamsin grew up on a stormy coast, so she picks storms. Her highest rank is 4, so her Passion rank is 6.

**Feat, hit points and equipment.** She picks the Cantrips feat. As a Wordweaver, she has a d6 hit die, so she starts with 12 hit points. She takes a dagger (d4), wears no armor, and has 3 empty spell slots.

**Everything else.** She adds +3 to initiative from her Move rank, and her passive Reveal is 3, so she notices carelessly hidden things without trying. Her native Tongue is the Drowned Tongue, from her stormy coast, which gives her its perk. She also speaks Hearthspeech well, but without its perk.

## Wordweaver
Wordweavers are scholars of language who believe every word holds power. Where others see a pile of letters, a Wordweaver sees a dozen spells waiting to be spoken. They choose their strongest ranks, have the most saved spells, and control their tiles the best, but they rely almost entirely on magic.

##### The Wordweaver

| Level | Features | Torrent of Words |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Torrent of Words, Tile Craft, Deep Study | 2 |
| 2 | Subclass feature | 2 |
| 3 | Overchannel | 2 |
| 4 | — | 2 |
| 5 | Subclass feature | 3 |
| 6 | Blended Casting | 3 |
| 7 | — | 3 |
| 8 | — | 3 |
| 9 | Echo | 4 |
| 10 | Subclass feature | 4 |
| 11 | — | 4 |
| 12 | Words of Making | 4 |

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 12 hit points (6 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d6 or take 4, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can't wear armor. You can use light weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 3 ranks to any schools of your choice.

### Torrent of Words
Words pour out of you faster than you can think them. As a bonus action during your pre-turn, before you roll, you can start a Torrent of Words. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed.

While it lasts, every die in your casting pool that rolls a 4 or higher counts as a surge, instead of 5 or higher. Your level is still capped by your rank.

You can use Torrent of Words a number of times shown in the Wordweaver table, and regain all uses after a long rest.

### Level 1: Tile Craft
Once per round, after your pre-turn, you can swap up to 2 of your tiles for random tiles from the pile.

### Level 1: Deep Study
You have one extra spell slot.

### Level 3: Overchannel
Once per round, after a successful pre-turn, you can raise your spell's level by 1, up to your rank. You take 1d8 damage when the spell takes effect.

\column

### Level 6: Blended Casting
During your pre-turn, you can name two schools and roll the casting pool of the lower-ranked one. If your spell fits both purposes, it does both, such as a blast that harms a foe and hurls them back.

### Level 9: Echo
Once per combat, after casting a spell, you can skip your next pre-turn and cast the same spell again at the same level on your next casting turn.

### Level 12: Words of Making
Once per long rest, a spell you cast takes effect at level 8, however many surges you rolled.

### Wordweaver Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Word Hoard.** Tile Craft lets you swap up to 4 tiles.
- **Arcane Recovery.** Once per long rest, after a short rest, roll to grow one of your saved spells as if you'd just cast it.
- **Long Words (4+).** A spell containing a word of 8 or more letters takes effect 1 level higher, up to your rank.
- **Sculpt Spell (4+).** Your spells that affect an area can leave out any creatures you choose.
- **Multicast (8+).** If your tiles spell two separate spells, you can cast both on one casting turn. Each takes effect 1 level lower, to a minimum of 1.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Lexicographer
Lexicographers collect words the way others collect coins. The longer and more precise the word, the stronger their magic.

- **Level 2: Well-Read.** Whenever you draw tiles for a new spell, draw 2 extra tiles, then return any 2 tiles to the pile.
- **Level 5: Precise Wording.** If your spell has 7 or more letters, its effect dice are one size bigger: d6s become d8s, and d8s become d10s.
- **Level 10: Unabridged.** Once per combat, if your spell uses every tile you have, with at least 6 tiles, it takes effect 2 levels higher, up to your rank.

\page

#### Devotee
Devotees give themselves over to what they love. Their passion burns brighter than any other caster's, and some even find room in their hearts for a second one.

- **Level 2: Second Passion.** Choose a second passion. You can cast Passion spells that tie into either of your passions, using your normal Passion rank.
- **Level 5: Burning Passion.** Your Passion spells' effect dice are one size bigger, so d6s become d8s. This adds up with Spell Focus.
- **Level 10: Kindred Passions.** Once per combat, a Passion spell that ties into both of your passions takes effect 2 levels higher, up to level 8.

#### Archivist

In a fight, they're slower than other casters, but their spells can't be heard coming.

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light and simple weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 1 rank to Change, 1 to Reveal, and 1 to any school of your choice.

**Lettered.** Your mundane Reveal actions to read, decipher or spot writing, symbols and traps have a mortal limit of 4.

### Written Word
You can cast spells by writing them instead of speaking them. Whenever you cast a spell, you can choose to write it.

- **What you need.** A free hand and something to write on. A quill and parchment works, but so does a finger in the dust or a knife on a tabletop. With floating ink, you can write in the air instead (see *Inks*).
- **Silent.** A written spell makes no sound, unless the DM decides it does, such as when you carve it into stone. Either way, it can't be countered with the Prepare action, and it works while you're silenced or gagged. If it does make a sound, it can still give away your position. Its range is the same as if it was spoken.
- **Slower.** Writing takes longer than speaking. A written spell takes effect at the end of the round, after every casting turn. If you're downed before then, the spell is left unfinished and doesn't work.

\column

### Runes
Instead of casting a written spell right away, you can leave it as a rune on a surface or object you can touch. When you write it, choose how it's triggered:

- **Released.** Whoever holds the object can release the spell as a bonus action. This lets you hand runes to your allies.
- **Set.** The spell takes effect when something happens, such as the rune being touched, read, or stepped over.

A rune fades once its spell takes effect, or after your next long rest if it hasn't. You can have a number of runes at once shown in the Runesmith table. Runes are hard to spot: a rune has a concealment level equal to the level of the spell it holds (see *Passive Reveal*).

You can also write runes outside of combat, rolling a pre-turn and spelling them as normal. Because you can take your time and try again, runes you write outside of combat hold spells up to level 2.

### Level 1: Drafting
Writing gives you time to think. When you write a spell, you can swap 1 of your tiles for a random tile from the pile before you reveal it.

### Level 3: Thrown Ink
You can write spells and runes on surfaces and objects you can see within 30 feet, instead of only ones you can touch.

### Level 6: Deep Runes
Runes you write outside of combat can hold spells up to level 4.

### Level 9: Fluent Hand
Your written spells now take effect on your casting turn, like spoken spells. You can still choose to have one take effect at the end of the round instead, such as to wait for an enemy to step into place.

### Level 12: Grand Inscription
Runes you write outside of combat can hold spells up to your rank. Once per long rest, you can release or trigger all your runes on a single casting turn.

\page

### Runesmith Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Shorthand.** A written spell of 6 letters or fewer takes effect on your casting turn, like a spoken spell.
- **Trigger Word.** As a bonus action, you can trigger any of your runes within 60 feet of you, whatever trigger you chose for it.
- **Rune Ammunition (4+).** A rune you've put on a weapon is released for free when that weapon's attack hits.
- **Deep Carving (4+).** Your runes can each be used twice before they fade.
- **Living Workshop (8+).** You can have 2 more runes at once. Your runes don't fade after a long rest, and when one is used, it recharges after your next long rest instead of fading.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Alchemist
Alchemists write their spells into potions instead of objects, brewing remedies and poisons for every occasion.

- **Level 2: Bottled Words.** During a long rest, roll your Change casting pool, draw tiles as normal, and spell up to as many words as your Change rank. Each word becomes a potion that holds that word's effect at level 1, in any school except Passion: a bottle of FROST, GLUE or CALM. Anyone can use a potion as a bonus action. **Drink it**, and its effect works on the drinker and lasts for 10 minutes instead of its usual duration. **Throw it** up to 30 feet, and it bursts where it lands, and every creature within 5 feet gets the potion's full effect. Your potions spoil after your next long rest.
- **Level 5: Volatile Mixtures.** As a bonus action, you can combine two of your potions and drink or throw them together. They take effect as a single level 2 effect that mixes both words, such as FIRE and GLUE making sticky, burning tar. If the DM decides the words clash, roll a d6: on a 1 or 2, the mixture explodes in your hands for 1d6 damage per level the mixture would have had.
- **Level 10: Master Brewer.** Your potions hold level 2 effects, and your mixtures level 3. You can mix up to three potions at once, and thrown potions burst across 15 feet instead of 5. Once per long rest, one potion you brew can hold its effect at your Change rank.

\column

#### Runewarden
Runewardens turn rooms into traps and battlefields into puzzles. Few things walk out of a place they've prepared.

- **Level 2: Lasting Runes.** You can have 1 more rune at once. Your runes don't fade after a long rest, and last for a week instead.
- **Level 5: Chain Runes.** When one of your set runes is triggered, you can trigger another of your runes within 30 feet of it.
- **Level 10: Warding Circle.** Once per long rest, as your action, you can draw a circle of runes up to 30 feet across around you, wherever you are. #### Runecarver
Runecarvers write on the things they carry into battle. Their blades and armor are covered in words, ready to be spoken with every swing.

- **Level 2: Carved Arms.** You can wear medium armor and use martial weapons. Your weapon and your armor can each hold one rune that doesn't count toward your limit.
- **Level 5: Bound Runes.** When a rune on your weapon or armor is used, it recharges after a short rest instead of fading.
- **Level 10: Words of Steel.** Once per combat, a rune on your weapon or armor takes effect 2 levels higher, up to your rank.

### Level 1: Tinker's Eye
Things you make with Create are one quality step better than their spell level, though not any bigger.

### Level 3: Field Repairs
Your mundane Change and Create actions can repair your construct, healing it like first aid but with d8s instead of d4s. It regains hit points during rests like you do.

Your construct now attacks with a casting pool of rank 3 and a d8 weapon die, and can have a second module.

### Level 9: Masterwork

### Level 12: Grand Engine

Shadows are assassins and scouts. By the time anyone notices them, the fight is usually over.

- **Level 2: Silent Step.** Your mundane Move actions for sneaking have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to 5. When you whisper a spell, it doesn't give away your position.
- **Level 5: Assassinate.** In the first round of combat, your Ambush deals its highest possible damage instead of rolling.
- **Level 10: Death Mark.** Once per combat, mark a creature you can see. Until the end of the combat, your Ambush dice against it are doubled.

#### Mimic
Mimics steal voices and words as easily as coins. They can sound like anyone, and cast like anyone too.

- **Level 2: Perfect Mimicry.** You can perfectly imitate any voice you've heard. Creatures who hear a spell you cast in someone else's voice believe that person cast it. If you cast it in a Tongue that person doesn't know, anyone who knows them can tell it's a fake.
- **Level 5: Copycat.** Once per combat, after an enemy reveals a spell in a Tongue you know or a written spell, you can cast that same spell on your next casting turn. You roll the original school of magic to get its level, and don't use tiles.
- **Level 10: Thousand Faces.** While Thrown Voice lasts, you can also look like any humanoid you've seen. You gain one extra use of Thrown Voice.

#### Cutthroat
Cutthroats don't wait for an opening. They make one, with a kick to the knee or a bottle to the head, and they don't stop until the fight is over.

- **Level 2: Low Blow.** Once per round, when your weapon attack deals damage, you can trip or disarm the target without rolling, if the DM agrees it's possible. A tripped target rolls one die fewer in its next casting pool. A disarmed target drops its weapon, and can't make weapon attacks with it until it picks it back up. Your Ambush also works against a creature that's knocked down, grabbed or disarmed.
- **Level 5: Vicious.** When you roll your Ambush dice, reroll any 1s, once. A creature your Ambush damages starts bleeding: it takes 1d4 damage at the start of each of its turns, until it's healed, someone uses an action to bandage it, or the combat ends. Bleeding doesn't stack.
- **Level 10: Carve a Path.** When you make a weapon attack, you can move up to your speed and attack every enemy you pass next to, with one pre-turn roll for all of them. Roll the damage once. Your first target takes all of it, plus your Ambush. Every other target takes half the damage, rounded down, without your Ambush.

## Spellblade
Spellblades are warriors who carve magic into every swing. They're as comfortable in the thick of a fight as any soldier, but their blade speaks words of fire and frost with every hit. Where other casters keep their distance, a Spellblade runs straight at the enemy.

##### The Spellblade

| Level | Features | Spellfury |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Spellfury, Weapon Training, Bladeword | 2 |
| 2 | Subclass feature | 2 |
| 3 | Parry | 2 |

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 16 hit points (10 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d10 or take 6, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light and medium armor. You can use all weapons, including heavy ones.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 2 ranks to Harm and 1 to Move.

### Spellfury
Magic floods your blade and your body. As a bonus action during your pre-turn, you can enter a Spellfury.

While it lasts, your weapon attacks deal one extra weapon die of damage, and you take half damage from spells.

Your Spellfury lasts until the end of the combat. It ends early if you're downed, or if a whole round passes in which you neither made a successful weapon attack nor took damage. You can also end it whenever you like.

You can use Spellfury a number of times shown in the Spellblade table, and regain all uses after a long rest.

### Level 1: Weapon Training
Your weapon attacks have a mortal limit of 4.

### Level 1: Bladeword
When you make a weapon attack, draw tiles equal to your highest die. If the attack deals damage, you can spell a word with them. If the word fits a school you have at rank 2 or higher, it adds that school's effect at level 1, such as FIRE for an extra 1d8 damage, or HALT to hold your target in place.

\column

### Level 3: Parry
Once per round, when you're hit, you can reduce the damage by a roll of your weapon die. If you're holding a shield, roll your weapon die twice and add the rolls together.

### Level 6: Weapon Mastery
Your weapon die is one size bigger, so a heavy weapon rolls a d12. Your weapon attacks have a mortal limit of 5, and your Bladewords take effect at level 2.

### Level 9: Cleave
When your weapon attack drops an enemy to 0 hit points, deal half its damage to another enemy next to you.

### Level 12: Spellstorm
Your Bladewords take effect at the same level as your weapon attack, and spread out from your target: they also affect every enemy within 10 feet of it.

### Spellblade Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Duelist.** You gain +1 armor while you fight with a single one-handed weapon and nothing in your other hand.
- **Charged Blade.** Instead of spelling a Bladeword, you can keep the tiles you drew for it, even when your attack misses. Add them to the tiles you draw for your next Bladeword. Kept tiles return to the pile at the end of the combat.
- **Arcane Riposte (4+).** When Parry reduces a hit's damage to 0, you can immediately make a level 1 weapon attack against the attacker.
- **Eldritch Reach (4+).** Magic carries your blade further. Your melee weapon attacks can hit a target up to 15 feet away.
- **Battle Caster (8+).** Once per combat, you can declare a weapon attack and a saved spell in the same pre-turn, and do both on your casting turn.

\page

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Heartblade
Heartblades fight with their passion in their hands. Their weapon takes on the shape of what they love most: a cutlass for a lover of the sea, a bone blade for someone fascinated by the dead.

- **Level 2: Passion Weapon.** Once per round, when your weapon attack hits, you can add 1d6 damage as a Passion effect, or heal an ally next to you for 1d6 instead. Like any Passion spell, the DM may ask how it ties into your passion.
- **Level 5: Heartfelt Bladewords.** Bladewords that tie into your passion take effect 1 level higher.
- **Level 10: Passion Unleashed.** While Spellfury lasts, you can roll your Passion casting pool for weapon attacks instead of your Harm casting pool. Your weapon attacks' mortal limit still applies.

#### Arcane Archer
Arcane Archers pour magic into their bow and arrows, striking harder and from further away than any other Spellblade.

- **Level 2: Enchanted Arrows.** Your ranged weapon attacks reach twice as far and ignore cover, and your ranged weapon die is one size bigger: a longbow rolls a d10.
- **Level 5: Volley.** Once per round, your ranged weapon attack can target two creatures. Roll its damage separately for each. Your Bladeword only affects the first target that is hit.
- **Level 10: Sky-Piercer.** Once per combat, your ranged weapon attack hits every creature in a line up to 120 feet long, and its Bladeword affects all of them.

#### Spellguard
Spellguards use their blade as a shield against magic, turning enemy spells aside and stealing their power.

- **Level 2: Spell Parry.** You gain Parry at level 2 instead of 3, and it can also reduce damage from spells.
- **Level 5: Absorb.** When Parry reduces a spell's damage to 0, draw tiles equal to the damage it prevented. You can keep them until the end of your next casting turn.
- **Level 10: Reflect.** Once per combat, when a spell targets only you, roll your Harm casting pool. If you roll at least as many surges as the spell's level, the spell targets its caster instead.

## Warden
Wardens are protectors who stand between their friends and whatever wants to hurt them. Clad in heavy armor and quick with a bandage, a Warden keeps the party standing when everything goes wrong. Their magic leans toward protection and healing, but their shield arm does just as much work.

##### The Warden

| 2 | Subclass feature | 2 |

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 18 hit points (12 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d12 or take 7, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear all armor and use shields. You can use all weapons, including heavy ones.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 2 ranks to Aid and 1 to Harm.

### Hold the Line
You plant your feet and raise your shield. As a bonus action during your pre-turn, you can start to Hold the Line. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed.

While it lasts, allies next to you take cover behind your shield: at the start of each round, every ally next to you gains a ward that absorbs 2 damage, which lasts until the start of the next round. Enemies roll one die fewer in their casting pool when they target anyone but you.

You can use Hold the Line a number of times shown in the Warden table, and regain all uses after a long rest.

### Level 1: Bulwark
Your weapon attacks and mundane Aid actions, such as first aid, have a mortal limit of 4.

### Level 1: Interpose
Once per round, when an ally next to you is hit, you can take the hit instead. Your armor applies.

\column

### Level 3: Steadfast
Once per long rest, when you would drop to 0 hit points, you drop to 1 instead.

### Level 6: Aegis
Anyone you protect with an Aid spell or mundane Aid action also gains a ward that absorbs damage equal to your armor value, until your next turn (see *Wards*). Your mundane Aid actions now have a mortal limit of 5.

### Level 9: Immovable
Change, Move and Control effects with a level lower than your Aid rank don't affect you, unless you allow them to.

### Level 12: Last Bastion
Once per long rest, for one full round, every hit against an ally within 10 feet of you hits you instead, at half damage, rounded down. Your armor then applies to each hit as normal.

### Warden Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Shield Bash.** When you use Interpose, you can also make a level 1 weapon attack against the attacker.
- **Field Medic.** Your first aid can reach allies within 15 feet of you, and heals with d6s instead of d4s.
- **Taunt (4+).** When your mundane Control action succeeds, the target must attack you on its next turn. An enemy can ignore this by rolling higher than the action's level + 2 on its resist die.
- **Iron Hide (4+).** You gain +1 armor.
- **Guardian Angel (8+).** You can use Interpose twice per round, for any ally you can reach.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Sentinel
Sentinels hold their ground so well that enemies can't get past them, or away from them.

- **Level 2: Hold Fast.** When your weapon attack hits a creature, it can't move away from you until the end of your next casting turn, unless it rolls higher than the attack's level + 2 on its resist die.
- **Level 5: Opportunist.** Once per round, when an enemy next to you attacks someone other than you, you can make a level 1 weapon attack against it.
- **Level 10: Wall of Iron.** While Hold the Line lasts, enemies can't move past you or through the space around you.

\page

#### Lifewarden
Lifewardens are healers in heavy armor, keeping their friends alive through sheer stubbornness.

- **Level 2: Healing Hands.** As a bonus action, you can give first aid at level 1 to an ally next to you. Your Aid spells heal 1 extra hit point for every effect die. If you share the dice between several creatures, each creature heals 1 extra for each die it gets.
- **Level 5: Shared Strength.** When you heal an ally with Aid, you also heal half as many hit points yourself.
- **Level 10: Second Chance.** Once per long rest, you can touch a creature that died within the last minute. It returns to life with 1 hit point.

#### Oathkeeper
Oathkeepers swear vows against those who harm their friends, and chase them down until the vow is kept.

- **Level 2: Vow.** Once per combat, mark an enemy you can see. Your weapon attacks and Harm spells against it deal an extra 1d6 damage until the end of the combat.
- **Level 5: Retribution.** When an enemy hits an ally next to you, you can make it your Vow target instead. Your next weapon attack against it is 1 level higher, up to your mortal limit.
- **Level 10: Unbroken Oath.** While your Vow target is alive, you drop to 1 hit point instead of 0 the first time you would fall in that combat.

## Feats
Feats are special talents that set your character apart: a knack for persuasion, a voice that shakes the walls, or a way with letters that other casters envy.

### Gaining Feats
You gain one feat at level 1, and another every time you level up. At levels 4, 8 and 12, you gain two feats instead of one. By level 12, you'll have 15 feats.

### Choosing Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of the general feats in this chapter, or one of the feats from your class's chapter.

- **Level requirements.** Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require you to be at least that level.
- **Once only.** You can only take each feat once, unless it says otherwise.
- **Specific beats general.** If a feat or class feature contradicts a general rule, the feat or feature wins. If two of them contradict each other, the DM decides.

### Body and Mind Feats
- **Alert.** Add 5 to your initiative rolls. You can't be caught unaware, so a Trickster's Ambush only works on you if you're distracted or have one of their allies next to you.
- **Athlete.** Add one die to mundane Move actions for climbing, jumping and swimming. You climb at full speed.
- **Fleet-Footed.** You move 10 feet further, and rough ground doesn’t slow you down.
- **Hard to Kill.** Your death rolls succeed on 8 or higher instead of 10. When you reach three successes, you wake up right away with 1 hit point instead of after an hour.
- **Lucky.** Once per long rest, you can reroll your whole casting pool.
- **Tough.** Your maximum hit points increase by 2 for every level you have, and by 2 more every time you level up. You can take this feat twice.
- **Steady Mind (4+).** Add one die to your casting pool when you make a saving throw.

### Combat Feats
- **Charger.** If you move at least 20 feet straight toward a creature before a melee weapon attack, the attack deals one extra weapon die.
- **Dual Wielder.** While you hold a light weapon in each hand, once per round after a successful weapon attack, you can make a level 1 attack with your other weapon. The target can be the same one or another creature next to you, and the attack needs no extra roll.
- **Heavy Armor Training.** You can wear heavy armor. You must already be able to wear medium armor.
- **Martial Training.** You can use martial weapons and wear medium armor.
- **Savage Attacker.** Once per round, you can reroll a weapon attack’s damage and use either total.
- **Shield Adept.** You can use shields.
- **War Caster (4+).** When you make an opportunity attack, you can cast one of your saved spells at the creature instead.

### Skill Feats
- **Artisan.** Choose one set of tools. Your mundane actions with them have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to 5, and take half the time. You can take this feat several times.
- **Keen Senses.** Add one die to your casting pool for mundane Reveal actions, such as searching a room or tracking someone.
- **Medic.** When you take a short rest, you and every ally resting with you heal extra hit points equal to your level. Your first aid can also cure poison and disease.
- **Observant.** Your passive Reveal is 1 higher.
- **Persuasive.** Add one die to your casting pool for mundane Control actions, such as persuading, deceiving or intimidating someone.
- **Stealthy.** Add one die to mundane Move actions for sneaking and hiding.
- **Trained.** Choose a school other than Passion. Your mundane actions in that school have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to a maximum of 5. You can take this feat several times, for different schools or the same one.

### Spellcraft Feats
- **Extra Slot.** You gain one extra spell slot. You can take this feat several times.
- **Polyglot.** You learn another Tongue, which you speak well. If you already speak the Tongue well, you become fluent in it and gain its perk. You can take this feat several times.
- **Lingering Magic (4+).** Once per combat, an effect that would last until the end of your next turn lasts one more round.
- **Signature Spell (4+).** Choose one of your saved spells. Once per combat, you can cast it at its saved level without rolling during your pre-turn. If you forget the spell, you can choose a new one after a long rest.
- **Spell Focus (4+).** Choose Harm, Aid or Passion. Spells of that school roll effect dice one size bigger: d6s become d8s, and d8s become d10s. You can take this feat several times, for different schools.
- **Twin Spell (8+).** Once per combat, a spell that targets one creature can target a second creature as well, rolling the same effect dice for each.
- **Reserve of Power (8+).** Once per combat, you can take a second pre-turn and a second casting turn in the same round, right after your first.

\page

### Tile Feats
- **Cantrips.** As a bonus action, you can spell a word of 3 letters or fewer with your leftover tiles. It takes effect at level 1, in any school except Harm and Passion.
- **Letter Hoard.** At the end of every casting turn, you can keep up to 3 unused tiles, or more if you couldn't spell anything.
- **Wild Letters (4+).** Once per combat, roll a d6 and replace that many of your tiles with blank tiles.

### Voice Feats
- **Booming Voice.** When you shout a spell, every enemy within 10 feet of you is deafened until the end of your next casting turn. Deafened creatures can't hear spells, so they can't counter them.
- **Carrying Voice.** Obstacles and noise never make you quieter for creatures you choose. Your allies can always hear you, even through a closed door or over a storm.
- **Soft-Spoken.** Your whispered spells reach up to 30 feet, but can still only be heard within 5 feet of you.
- **Lip Reader.** You can counter spells cast by creatures you can see, even if you can't hear them, as long as you know the spell's Tongue.
- **Commanding Voice (4+).** When you shout a Control spell, it can affect one extra creature within range, without needing a higher level.
- **Silent Casting (8+).** Once per combat, you can cast a spell by only mouthing the words. It reaches as far as a spoken spell, but makes no sound, so it can't be heard. Only a creature with Lip Reader that can see you and knows the spell's Tongue can counter it. It takes effect 1 level lower, to a minimum of 1.

## Advancement
As your character adventures, they grow more skilled and more powerful. This is shown by their **level**, which goes from 1 to 12.

### Gaining Levels
Spellbound uses milestones: you level up when your DM says so, usually after an important moment in the story, such as clearing a dungeon, defeating a villain, or completing a quest. You don't track experience points.

### Leveling Up
Every time you gain a level, do the following:

1. **Hit points.** Roll your class's hit die, or take the fixed number shown in your class's chapter, and add it to your maximum hit points.
2. **Rank point.** Add 1 rank to any school except Passion. You can't go over the rank cap for your new level.
3. **Feats.** Choose one feat, or two at levels 4, 8 and 12 (see *Feats*).
4. **Class and subclass features.** Check your class's table for new features, and for anything that grows, such as extra uses of your signature ability, more runes, or a stronger construct.
5. **Spell slots.** Check the table below to see if you gain a spell slot.

Your Passion rank follows your highest other rank, so it rises on its own as your other ranks do.

##### Advancement

| Level | Rank cap | Spell slots | Feats gained |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 4 | 3 | 1 |
| 4 | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| 5 | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| 6 | 6 | 4 | 1 |
| 7 | 6 | 5 | 1 |
| 8 | 7 | 5 | 2 |
| 9 | 7 | 6 | 1 |
| 10 | 7 | 6 | 1 |
| 11 | 8 | 7 | 1 |
| 12 | 8 | 7 | 2 |

Wordweavers have one extra spell slot at every level, from their Deep Study feature.

\column

### Retraining
Characters change as they grow. Whenever you level up, you can also swap one of your feats for another feat you qualify for. Your DM may also let you move one rank point from one school to another, if your character has a good story reason for it.

### Starting at a Higher Level
If your DM asks you to start above level 1, create a level 1 character first, then level up one level at a time until you reach the starting level. For hit points, take the fixed number each time instead of rolling.

## Running Enemies
*This chapter is for the DM.*

Enemies follow the same rules as player characters. They take pre-turns and casting turns, roll casting pools, count surges, and spell their spells out loud, where the players can hear and counter them. This chapter shows how to build enemies quickly and keep fights fair as the party grows.

### Party Level
Enemies are built around the **party level (PL)**: the average level of the player characters, rounded down. A party of three level 3 characters and one level 5 has a party level of 3.

### Tiers
Every enemy belongs to one of four tiers. Use the table to find its numbers.

##### Enemy Stats

| Tier | Hit points | Armor | Resist die | Weapon die |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Minion | 3 + 2×PL | 0 | d6 | d6 |
| Standard | 8 + 5×PL | 0–1 | d6 | d8 |
| Elite | 15 + 8×PL | 1–2 | d8 | d10 |
| Boss | 30 + 15×PL | 2 | d10 (d12 if legendary) | d12 |

##### Best Rank and Guard DC

| Tier | PL 1–4 | PL 5–8 | PL 9–12 |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Minion | Rank 2, DC 3 | Rank 3, DC 3 | Rank 4, DC 4 |
| Standard | Rank 2, DC 4 | Rank 4, DC 5 | Rank 5, DC 6 |
| Elite | Rank 3, DC 5 | Rank 5, DC 6 | Rank 6, DC 7 |
| Boss | Rank 4, DC 6 | Rank 6, DC 7 | Rank 8, DC 8 |

- **Best rank** is the rank of the school the enemy is best at. Its other useful schools are 1 or 2 ranks lower, and everything else is rank 1.
- **Guard DC** is the default difficulty for spells and mundane actions aimed at the enemy. Raise it by 1 or 2 for a spell that suits the target poorly, and lower it for one that exploits a weakness.
- **Resist die** is what the enemy rolls to break free of Change, Move and Control effects (see *Lasting Effects*), and to resist hazards. Against a hazard, it resists if it equals or beats the DC.
- **Weapon die** is the die its weapon attacks roll, once per level.

\column

### Enemy Actions
- **Mortal limit.** Enemy mundane actions have a mortal limit of 3 for minions and standard enemies, 4 for elites, and 5 for bosses. A dragon's bite is still a mundane action: it's what a dragon does without magic.
- **Bosses act twice.** A boss takes two pre-turns and two casting turns every round, at two different points in the initiative order. Roll initiative for it twice.
- **Signature tricks.** Give elites and bosses one special ability, much like a class's signature ability. An ogre might stamp the ground and knock everyone near it prone, or a lich might drain a tile from every player who hears it.

### Enemy Spells
Spelling for every enemy slows the game down. Instead, give each enemy that casts spells 1 to 3 **known spells**, like a player's saved spells: a goblin shaman's STING, a cultist's CURSE, a lich's WITHER. Known spells start at level 1 or 2, and up to the enemy's rank for elites and bosses.

Enemies cast known spells the same way players cast saved spells: they roll their pool during their pre-turn, draw no tiles, and cast at the known spell's level.

Give every enemy that casts spells a native **Tongue**. It decides whether players can copy, steal or lip-read its magic, and describing how it sounds tells the players what they're up against.

Save real spelling for bosses, where the drama is worth it. Draw tiles for a boss like any player, and spell its words aloud at the table. 

Enemies speak their spells, so the rules for *Volume* and *What Blocks Sound* apply to them too. A cultist whispering behind a door can't be countered by anyone who can't hear them.

### Building Encounters
A fair fight costs one point of **budget** per player character.

| Tier | Cost |
|:--- |:--- |
| Minion | ½ |
| Standard | 1 |
| Elite | 2 |
| Boss | 4 |

- **Fair fight.** 1 point per player.
- **Hard fight.** 1.5 points per player.
- **Deadly fight.** 2 or more points per player. Someone will probably go down, and might not get back up.

A party of four facing a fair fight might meet four standard enemies, or one elite and four minions. A boss fight for the same party might be one boss with two minions.

\page

### Using *Dungeons & Dragons* Monsters
You can use monsters from *Dungeons & Dragons* adventures by translating them:

1. **Tier.** Pick the tier that matches the monster's role: weak swarming creatures are minions, ordinary foes are standard, tough foes are elites, and the big villain is a boss.
2. **Numbers.** Use the tier table for hit points, armor, ranks, Guard DC, resist die and weapon die. Don't convert the original numbers.
3. **Attacks.** Each attack becomes a mundane Harm action with the tier's weapon die.
4. **Spells and abilities.** Turn the monster's spells into known spells, and its most memorable ability into a signature trick. Give it a native Tongue that fits where it comes from.

## Judging Spells
*This chapter is for the DM.*

In Spellbound, every spell is a small negotiation between the player and the DM. This chapter helps you make those calls quickly and fairly, so the game keeps moving.

### Three Questions
When a player reveals a spell, ask yourself three things:

1. **Are they real words?** Every word in the spell has to be real, or the player has to accept a backfire for bending the rules.
2. **Do the words fit the goal?** The spell should make sense for what the player wants to do. FREEZE fits stopping a guard in his tracks. CHEESE probably doesn't, unless the player has a very good story.
3. **Does the goal fit the school and level?** The goal has to match the school they rolled, and its size has to match the level (see *Spell Levels*).

If all three are a yes, the spell works. Don't spend long on it: a quick yes keeps the table excited.

### Generous Words, Strict Levels
Be generous with words and strict with levels. Players should feel free to be creative with what they spell, because the level already limits how powerful it can be. A player who spells DEATH at level 1 doesn't kill anything, but you can let them deal 1d8 damage with a chill that feels like death.

If a spell is a stretch but the player's explanation is fun, you can let the spell work at 1 level lower instead of saying no.

### Common Rulings
- **Word forms.** Plurals, verb tenses and other forms of a word all count. FROZE is as good as FREEZE.
- **Names.** Names of people, places and creatures count if they exist in your world. The player can't just invent one.
- **Other languages.** Words from other real-world languages count as bending the rules, unless your table agrees to allow them. Tongues don't change this: they decide whose magic you can read, not which words count.
- **Killing words.** Words like DIE or KILL never kill outright. They deal damage like any Harm spell.
- **The same spell again.** Players can spell the same spell every turn if they draw the letters. If one spell starts to dominate, remind them they could save it in one of their spell slots instead.
- **Two schools at once.** If a spell seems to fit two schools, it only does what the school they rolled allows. A FIREBALL rolled with Move might knock a goblin back, but it won't burn it.

\column

### Judging Passion
Passion is powerful, so it should always need a reason. Before you approve a Passion spell, ask the player why their passion would be here. An undead passion in a graveyard is easy. An undead passion in the middle of the sea needs a sunken shipwreck, or a drowned sailor, or something just as good.

Reward players who plan ahead. A character passionate about music who carries a lute is easier to approve than one who doesn't.

### Backfires
When a player bends the rules, choose a backfire that fits how they bent them, or roll on this table.

| d8 | Backfire |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | **Recoil.** You take 1d4 damage per spell level. |
| 2 | **Echo.** The spell also affects you, at half strength. |
| 3 | **Tongue-tied.** You roll one die fewer in your next casting pool. |
| 4 | **Wild aim.** The spell hits a random creature within range instead. |
| 5 | **Too loud.** Everyone within 300 feet hears the spell, as if you'd shouted it. |
| 6 | **Drained.** You draw 3 fewer tiles the next time you draw. |
| 7 | **Twisted.** The spell works, but in a strange way the DM chooses, inspired by the misspelling. |
| 8 | **Odd side effect.** The spell works, and something harmless but strange happens: your hair turns blue, or everything smells of onions for an hour. |

### Keeping the Game Moving
- **Spell during pre-turns.** Remind players to work on their words while others take their pre-turns, not when their casting turn comes up.
- **Use a timer.** If casting turns drag, give each player a minute to reveal their spell. When time runs out, they keep tiles as if they couldn't spell.
- **Let the table help.** Other players can suggest words, but the caster decides. Helping each other spell is part of the fun.
- **Don't punish clever spelling.** If a player finds a surprising word or phrase that fits perfectly, let it shine. That moment is what Spellbound is about.

### Example
Rowan rolls Harm at level 2 and spells ISE SPIKE, misspelling ICE. The word is bent, the goal fits Harm, and a level 2 spell has two dice to share, enough to hit two cultists. The DM approves the spell, so each cultist takes 1d8 damage. For the backfire, the DM picks one that fits the bent word: frost creeps up Rowan's fingers, and she takes 2d4 damage.

### The Core Loop
1. **Pre-turn.** Name a school, and whether you cast a new spell, a saved spell, or take a mundane action. Get a DC and roll your casting pool.

## Appendix B: Glossary
}}

**Backfire.** What goes wrong when you bend the rules to make a spell work. See *Bending the Rules* and *Judging Spells*.

**Bending the rules.** Misspelling a word or bending grammar to cast a spell, at the cost of a backfire.

**Bonus action.** A quick extra action alongside your main one, only available when a feature or item allows it. Most happen on your casting turn, some during your pre-turn. See *Bonus Actions*.

**Budget.** The points a DM spends on enemies when building an encounter: 1 point per player character makes a fair fight. See *Building Encounters*.

**Casting pool.** The dice you roll for a school, based on your rank in it. See *Casting Pools*.

**Casting turn.** The second part of every round, when you resolve what you chose in your pre-turn. See *Casting Turn*.

**Concealment level.** How well something is hidden, from 1 to 8. You notice it automatically if your passive Reveal equals or beats it.

**Construct.** A Golemwright's companion, built from scrap during a long rest. It acts on its creator's casting turn, right after them, and has the same Guard DC as its creator. See *Construct*.

**Death roll.** The d20 you roll every round while downed. See *Dropping to 0 Hit Points*.

**Difficulty (DC).** The number at least one die in your casting pool must equal or beat to succeed. See *Difficulty*.

**Downed.** At 0 hit points and unconscious.

**Effect dice.** The dice you roll for damage or healing: one die per level. A spell can share them between several creatures, at least one die each. See *Effect Dice*.

**Fluent.** Speaking a Tongue well enough to gain its perk. You're fluent in your native Tongue, and in any Tongue you've learned twice. See *Tongues*.

**Guard DC.** The default difficulty for actions aimed at a creature. An enemy's depends on its tier, and a player character's on their level. A construct uses its creator's. See *Running Enemies* and *Player Guard DC*.

**Hit points.** How much damage you can take before you're downed. See *Hit Points*.

**Known spell.** An enemy's version of a saved spell.

**Level (character).** How experienced your character is, from 1 to 12. See *Advancement*.

**Level (spell or action).** How powerful a spell or mundane action is: one level for every surge you rolled, with a minimum of 1. It can never be higher than your rank in that school, and a mundane action can't go above your mortal limit. A saved spell uses its saved level instead. See *Reading Your Roll* and *Spell Levels*.

**Module.** An upgrade built into a construct, such as Springs or a Ballista. A construct can have a number of modules shown in the Golemwright table. See *Modules*.

**Mortal limit.** The highest level a mundane action can reach: 3, unless a feature raises it.

**Mundane action.** A non-magical action, such as an attack or a persuasion attempt. It uses a school's casting pool but needs no tiles. See *Mundane Actions*.

**Next to you.** Within 5 feet of you. See *Distances*.

**Opportunity attack.** A weapon attack you can make as a reaction when an enemy moves away from you. See *Opportunity Attacks*.

**Party level (PL).** The average level of the player characters, rounded down. Used to build enemies.

**Passion.** The eighth school, tied to something your character loves. Its rank is your highest other rank + 2. See *Passion*.

**Passive Reveal.** Your Reveal rank, used to notice hidden things without rolling. See *Passive Reveal*.

**Pre-turn.** The first part of every round, when you choose what to do and roll your casting pool. See *Pre-Turn*.

**Prepare.** A pre-turn action that lets you counter one spell you can hear. See *Prepare*.

**Rank.** Your skill in a school, from 1 to 8. See *Ranks*.

**Resist die.** The die an enemy rolls to break free of effects and resist hazards.

**Rune.** A spell written on a surface or object, released by whoever holds it or set to trigger later. Used by Runesmiths.

**Saved spell.** A spell you've stored in a spell slot, which you can cast without tiles. See *Saving Spells*.

**Saving throw.** A roll of a school's casting pool to resist something that happens to you. See *Saving Throws*.

**Shield.** A piece of equipment that adds 1 to your armor value and takes up one hand. Only classes and feats that say so let you use one. A magical shield from an Aid spell is a ward instead. See *Armor and Shields*.

**Signature ability.** The ability that defines your class and that only it has, such as a Spellblade's Spellfury or a Golemwright's Construct. Most can be used a limited number of times per long rest. The Runesmith's Written Word and the Golemwright's Construct have no uses, and grow in other ways.

**Spell slot.** A space for one saved spell.

**Stabilize.** Saving a downed ally with a successful mundane Aid action. It counts as three death roll successes: they stop rolling, and wake up with 1 hit point after an hour. See *Dropping to 0 Hit Points*.

**Subclass.** A specialization within your class, chosen at level 2.

**Surge.** A die in your casting pool that rolls 5 or higher. Each surge adds 1 to your level.

**Tiles.** The letter tiles you draw to spell your spells.

\page

**Tongue.** The grammar a spell is cast in. Knowing a spell's Tongue lets you read or copy its magic, and each Tongue you're fluent in gives a small perk. See *Tongues*.

**Volume.** How loudly you cast a spell: whisper, speak or shout. It decides your spell's range and who hears it. See *Volume*.

**Ward.** Anything that absorbs damage for a creature, such as a magical barrier, a protective creature, or a Warden's shield. Wards come from Aid spells and some features. See *Wards*.

## Appendix E: Feature Interactions
*This appendix is for the DM.*

Many features change how other features work. When one of these situations comes up, check the creatures involved for the features listed. If two features still seem to clash, specific beats general, and the DM decides the rest.

### When a Spell Is Cast
- **Countering a spell** (Prepare, Countersong, Encore). Written spells can't be countered: check for Written Word and Written in Ink. Silent Casting can only be countered by a Lip Reader who sees the caster and knows its Tongue, and Thrown Voice only by creatures who see the Trickster. Deafened creatures can't counter, so check for Booming Voice.
- **Changing or stealing tiles** (Sleight, Twist the Word, Devour Words). Saved spells and known spells use no tiles, so these don't work on them.
- **Copying a spell** (Copycat, Perfect Heist). These work on enemies' known spells too, but only in a Tongue the Trickster knows. Written spells have no Tongue, so anyone can copy them.
- **Reading magic** (Lip Reader, Duet). Check that the creature knows the spell's Tongue. Written spells, runes, potions and constructs work for anyone.
- **Raising a spell's level** (Overchannel, Long Words, Unabridged, Heartfelt Bladewords, Legendary Tale, Annotated Spells, Words of Steel, Duet). Never above the caster's rank, except with Kindred Passions, Duet and Words of Making.
- **Bigger effect dice** (Spell Focus, Burning Passion, Precise Wording, Enchanted Arrows, Weapon Mastery, Colossus, Field Medic). Each makes the dice one size bigger, and they add up, up to a d12.
- **Sharing effect dice.** Healing Hymn and Healing Hands add 1 hit point for each die a creature gets. Thrown potions and Twin Spell give every creature the full dice.
- **Hearing a spell or song** (Battle Hymn, Siren Song, Shared Verse). Check *What Blocks Sound*, deafened creatures, and Carrying Voice.

### When a Creature Is Moved or Controlled
- **Breaking free** from Change, Move and Control effects. Enemies roll their resist die against the level + 2, or + 3 against Enchanting Voice. Players make a saving throw, and Steady Mind adds a die.
- **Effects on a Warden.** Immovable ignores Change, Move and Control effects below the Warden's Aid rank. Effects with a DC instead of a level, like Ground Stomp, still call for a saving throw.
- **Moving away** from an enemy. Check for opportunity attacks, War Caster, Slippery, Hold Fast and Wall of Iron.

\column

### Casting Pools and Limits
- **Adding and losing dice.** Adding: Battle Hymn, War Chant, Rallying Refrain, Steady Mind, Persuasive, Keen Senses, Athlete, Stealthy, Fanatic, Heavy Frame. Losing: Siren Song, Mocking Verse, Dirty Fighting, Low Blow, Hold the Line, Tongue-tied. They all add up, but a pool never drops below one die, and the level is still capped by rank.
- **Mortal limit** (Trained, Artisan, Expert, Silver tongue, Handy, Weapon Training, Weapon Mastery, Bulwark, Aegis, Battle Rhythm, Silent Step, Well of Stories, Sneaky, Lettered). These raise the limit, but never above 5. Retribution raises a weapon attack's level, but only up to the mortal limit.
- **Extra turns** (Echo, Reserve of Power, Battle Caster, Grand Library, Multicast, Conduit, Overdrive). Each extra action needs its own successful pre-turn roll, unless the feature says otherwise, like Dual Wielder and Carve a Path.

### When a Creature Is Hit
- **Ambush.** Doesn't work against Alert, unless the target is distracted or next to an ally of the Trickster. Thrown Voice, Silver Lies and Low Blow make it work anyway. Assassinate, Vanish, Death Mark and Vicious change its dice. Carve a Path only adds it to the first target.
- **Taking a hit for an ally** (Interpose, Guardian Angel, Last Bastion, Guardian Protocol). Only one creature can take each hit. Shield Bash and Retribution trigger when a Warden does.

- **Reducing damage.** Apply halving first (Spellfury against spells, Slippery against area spells, Uncanny Dodge, Last Bastion), then Parry, then wards, then armor. Armor never lowers a hit below 1. Only the strongest ward counts, so check Aegis, Hold the Line and Aid spells.
- **Dropping to 0 hit points** (Steadfast, Saga of Heroes, Unbroken Oath). Only one can stop a single fall, chosen by the creature that falls.
### When Someone Is Hidden
- **Casting while hidden.** A spoken spell gives the caster away. Check for Silent Step (whispered spells), Thrown Voice, Written Word and Silent Casting. Vanish ends when the Trickster attacks or casts a spoken spell.
- **Noticing hidden things.** Compare passive Reveal to the concealment level of runes, a Warding Circle, or a sneaking creature. Keen Senses adds a die when searching, and Observant raises passive Reveal by 1.

## Appendix F: Changes
What's changed between versions of Spellbound, newest first.

### Version 0.4
- **Tongues.** You're fluent in your native Tongue, and only speak your second Tongue well. Only Tongues you're fluent in give their perk. Polyglot teaches you a new Tongue, or makes you fluent in one you already speak.
- **Equipment.** Added *Tools* and *Inks*, including floating ink that can be drawn in the air.
- **Golemwright.** A construct has the same Guard DC as you. Springs lets a Warforger's construct move three times as far as you.
- **Archivist.** Annotated Spells rolls a die of the school's casting pool for a saved spell's starting level, at least 2 and up to your rank.
- **Spellblade.** Bladewords no longer have a letter limit: the tiles you draw are the limit. Spellstorm makes your Bladewords spread to every enemy within 10 feet of the target. Charged Blade now lets you keep your Bladeword tiles for your next Bladeword.
- **Trickster.** Dirty Fighting rolls one die of your Control casting pool against a DC set by the DM, who decides which effects are possible. Uncanny Dodge uses your reaction. The Cutthroat subclass replaces the Gambler.
- **Feats.** Added a Combat group with Charger, Dual Wielder, Heavy Armor Training, Savage Attacker and War Caster, and moved Martial Training and Shield Adept there. Added Athlete, Fleet-Footed and Hard to Kill to Body and Mind, Artisan, Observant and Stealthy to Skill, and Lingering Magic to Spellcraft.

### Version 0.3
- **Runesmith and Golemwright.** The Runesmith is split into two classes. Runesmiths write spells and runes, with the Alchemist, Runewarden and Runecarver subclasses. Golemwrights build constructs with modules, with the Clockmaker, Warforger and Puppeteer subclasses. Glyphs are now part of runes.
- **Player Guard DC.** Player characters have a Guard DC based on their level, used when enemies aim spells and mundane actions at them.
- **Wards.** Ward points are removed. Features that gave ward points now give wards, and a ward can be anything that absorbs damage, such as a magical barrier or a protective creature.
- **Signature abilities.** The Runesmith's Written Word and the Golemwright's Construct are signature abilities without uses.
- **Tongues.** Ashen's Old Authority no longer frightens creatures. Bargains sealed with Ashen magic bind both sides.
- **Countering.** Prepare now lowers a spell's level by one for each surge, like Countersong.
- **Loresinger.** Shared Verse can be used once per round, and Inspiring Word needs the ally to hear you.
- **Trickster.** Ambush dice are shown in the Trickster table. Vanish is a bonus action on your casting turn. Copycat and Perfect Heist also work on written spells.
- **Oathkeeper.** Retribution is capped by your mortal limit instead of your rank.
- **Enemies.** A deadly fight is 2 or more points of budget per player. The Cult Acolyte has Aid 2.
- **Glossary.** Added budget, hit points, shield and stabilize, and reworded Level and Signature ability.

### Version 0.2
- **Turn structure.** You get one bonus action per round, during your pre-turn or casting turn as the feature says. Added *Opportunity Attacks* and *Distances*: "next to you" means within 5 feet.
- **Magic in the world.** New section explaining what letter tiles are in the story.
- **Tongues.** Every spell is cast in a Tongue, the grammar of a magical tradition. Knowing a Tongue lets you lip-read, copy or steal magic in it, and gives a small perk. See *Tongues*.

- **Effect dice.** Effects that make dice bigger add up, up to a d12.
- **Once per long rest.** Every "once per session" feature is now once per long rest.

- **Several targets.** Damage, healing and wards no longer roll smaller dice for several targets. Instead, a spell shares its effect dice between creatures, at least one die each, so it can affect as many creatures as its level. See *Sharing Effect Dice*.

- **Healing Hymn and Healing Hands.** These now heal 1 extra hit point per effect die, so shared dice share the bonus too.
