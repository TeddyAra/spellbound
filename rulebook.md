## Introduction
Spellbound is a homebrew TTRPG ruleset that combines *Dungeons & Dragons* and *Spell: The RPG*. This ruleset is meant to allow for more creativity in casting spells by taking the spell creation mechanic from *Spell: The RPG* while adding general gameplay similar to *Dungeons & Dragons*.

Whenever a mechanic comes up that isn't explicitly stated in this ruleset, you should default to *Dungeons & Dragons* or make up a rule of your own. Spellbound relies heavily on improvising, both for the DM and the players, almost every round of combat. These rules will help, but do not hesitate to come up with fun ways to play your sessions yourself!

## What You Need
Besides what you normally need to play *Dungeons & Dragons*, playing Spellbound in person also requires *Scrabble tiles*. It is recommended to have as many as you can, especially with higher level players, to avoid running out of tiles or having to share them, which could create longer waiting times during combat.

As an alternative to playing Spellbound physically, you can use the [Spellbound Dashboard](https://teddyara.github.io/spellbound/). This dashboard allows you to roll dice to cast spells and create and move letter tiles.

## What Makes Spellbound Different
If you've played *Dungeons & Dragons*, most of Spellbound will feel familiar. A few things work very differently:

- **You make your own spells.** There's no spell list. You draw letter tiles and spell out whatever you want your magic to do, from STING to THE FLOOR TURNS TO ICE.
- **Schools describe goals, not methods.** A Harm spell can be a fireball, a falling boulder, or a swarm of bees. What matters is what the spell is meant to do.
- **Magic and skill use the same dice.** Every school has a mundane side, so the same Harm dice swing your sword, and the same Control dice talk your way past a guard.
- **Your dice grow with you.** Beginners roll a handful of d6s. Masters roll d8s, d10s and finally d12s, which can reach DCs no beginner ever could.
- **Your voice matters.** Spells are spoken out loud, so whispering, shouting, and who can hear you all change how a fight plays out.

## Turn Structure
Before combat starts, everyone rolls for initiative to decide the order of turns. Roll a d20 and add your Move rank. Whoever has the highest total goes first, then the next highest, and so on. If two creatures tie, the one with the higher Reveal rank goes first. If they're still tied, players go before enemies.

Initiative stays the same for the whole combat.

### Rounds
During combat, every round is split into two parts: a **pre-turn** and a **casting turn**. Everyone takes their pre-turn, in initiative order. Once the last person or enemy's pre-turn has ended, it loops back around to the first person or enemy's casting turn.

### Pre-Turn
During your pre-turn, you get to choose from a list of actions:
1. **Cast a spell.** When you take this action, you're choosing to either create a new spell or cast a spell from your saved spell slots.
2. **Perform a mundane action.** This covers most actions, replacing actions from *Dungeons & Dragons* such as attacking and making ability checks.
3. **Prepare.** When you take this action, you're forgoing your casting turn to be able to cast a counter-spell against any spell you can hear until your next turn. After casting the counter-spell, regardless of whether it was successful, your Prepare action has run out.

Besides these, you can choose to use any other actions as described in *Dungeons & Dragons*. These actions are almost always performed during the casting turn rather than the pre-turn, so you simply announce them during your pre-turn.

#### Rolling Your Pre-Turn
Casting a spell and performing a mundane action both start with a roll. Tell the DM which school of magic your action falls under, and the DM gives you a difficulty (DC) in return. Roll your casting pool for that school (see *Rolling the Dice*):

- If at least one die equals or beats the DC, your action succeeds.
- Count every die that rolled a 5 or higher. This is your action's **level**. If none did, the level is 1. The level can never be higher than your rank in that school.
- If you're creating a new spell, draw letter tiles equal to the total of your roll. Mundane actions and saved spells draw no tiles.

\column

If your roll fails, you draw nothing and your chosen action does nothing this round. You still get your casting turn for movement and any other actions.

Your pre-turn is short on purpose. Use the time while others take their pre-turns to work out your spell from your tiles.

#### Prepare
If you take the Prepare action, you don't roll during your pre-turn. Instead, when a creature you can hear reveals its spell, you may counter it. Roll your casting pool for the school of the spell you're countering. Each die that rolls a 5 or higher lowers that spell's level by 1. If the level drops below 1, the spell fizzles entirely.

You can only counter once per Prepare action. Preparing uses up your casting turn's action, but you can still move.

### Casting Turn
Once every pre-turn has been taken, casting turns begin, in the same initiative order. On your casting turn, you resolve whatever you chose in your pre-turn:

- **New spell.** Spell out your spell with your tiles and explain how it fits the school of magic. If the DM approves, the spell takes effect at the level you rolled.
- **Bending the rules.** You may misspell a word or bend grammar to make your spell work. The DM can still approve it, but expect a backfire. That could be damage, a complication, or something worse.
- **No spell.** If you can't come up with a spell, keep a number of tiles equal to your spell's level. You can use them on your next casting turn.
- **Saved spell.** If your pre-turn roll succeeded, the spell takes effect at its saved level. No spelling needed.
- **Mundane action.** Describe what you do. It takes effect at the level you rolled, up to the mortal limit (see *Mundane Actions*).

On your casting turn, you can also move and take any other actions *Dungeons & Dragons* allows, such as interacting with an object. Reactions work as normal and can be taken at any time.

#### Bonus Actions
Some features and items let you do something as a **bonus action**: a quick extra thing on your casting turn, alongside your main action, such as drinking a potion or a few quick words of magic. You get one bonus action per casting turn, and only if something lets you take one. A bonus action doesn't need a pre-turn roll unless it says otherwise.

At the end of your casting turn, return all used tiles and any tiles you didn't keep to the pile.

### Example Round
Mira, Bram and a goblin are in combat, in that initiative order.

**Pre-turns.** Mira wants to attack the goblin with magic. She chooses Harm, gets a DC of 4, and rolls her 3d6: 2, 4 and 5. Two dice meet the DC, so she succeeds. One die rolled a 5 or higher, so her spell is level 1, and she draws 11 tiles. Bram takes the Prepare action. The goblin chooses a mundane action to attack Mira with its spear and succeeds.

**Casting turns.** Mira spells STING and deals 1d8 damage to the goblin. Bram has nothing to counter yet, so he walks to Mira's side. The goblin stabs Mira. Since its attack isn't a spell, Bram's Prepare can't counter it, and his Prepare stays active until his next turn.

### Outside of Combat
Outside of combat, there are no pre-turns or casting turns. When you want to do something, tell the DM what you're trying and which school it falls under, then roll, spell, and resolve it in one go. Take as long as you need to spell, unless the DM says time is short.

## Schools of Magic
There are eight schools of magic. Rather than being split up by what kind of spells you'd perform in each school, each school describes a spell's purpose or goal. They are:
1. **Harm.** Any magic meant to damage, destroy, or weaken.
2. **Aid.** Any magic meant to protect, restore, or support.
3. **Change.** Any magic meant to alter the properties or nature of something.
4. **Move.** Any magic meant to move something.
5. **Control.** Any magic meant to direct the behavior of creatures or objects.
6. **Reveal.** Any magic meant to gather or enhance information.
7. **Create.** The permanent creation of an object, creature, or phenomenon, with no purpose of its own.
8. **Passion.** Any magic that ties into your chosen passion.

Each school of magic can also be used for mundane actions, which are actions that are non-magical. Examples include melee attacks with Harm, athletics rolls with Move, and persuasion with Control.

### Harm
Harm spells deal damage, break objects, or weaken a target. A Harm spell deals 1d8 damage per spell level. You can share these dice between several creatures, giving each at least one die, so a Harm spell can hit as many creatures as its level (see *Sharing Effect Dice*).

**Spell examples.** Shooting a fireball, exploding a wall, cursing a knight's armor to rust.

**Mundane examples.** Swinging a sword, shooting an arrow, throwing a punch.

### Aid
Aid spells heal, shield, or support creatures and objects. An Aid spell that heals restores 1d8 hit points per spell level, and you can share these dice between several creatures, giving each at least one die. An Aid spell that creates a ward absorbs 1d8 damage per spell level until your next turn. Like healing, you can share these dice between several creatures or objects, giving each its own ward of at least one die. Any other Aid spell effects are up to the discretion of the DM.

**Spell examples.** A shield around an ally, mending a broken wall, giving a tired friend a second wind.

**Mundane examples.** Bandaging a wound, treating poison, guarding an ally.

\page

### Change
Change spells alter what something is or how it behaves. The bigger the change, the higher the level it needs: turning a lock is easy, turning a castle to glass is not.

**Spell examples.** Unlocking a lock, turning water into ice, making a rope as hard as steel.

**Mundane examples.** Picking a lock, putting on a disguise, mixing a potion.

### Move
Move spells move creatures or objects. The heavier the thing or the further it goes, the higher the level it needs.

**Spell examples.** A gust of wind knocking someone over, summoning a giant to lift a boulder, pulling a key across the room.

**Mundane examples.** Climbing, jumping, shoving, throwing.

### Control
Control spells direct how creatures or objects behave. A creature affected against its will is controlled for a number of rounds equal to the spell's level. At the end of each of its turns, it may try to break free.

**Spell examples.** Making a guard let you through, vines wrapping around someone, stopping a set of gears.

**Mundane examples.** Persuading, deceiving, or intimidating someone.

### Reveal
Reveal spells give you information or sharpen your senses. A Reveal spell gives you one clear fact or detail per spell level, or a sense that lasts 10 minutes per spell level.

**Spell examples.** Night-vision, detecting magic, hearing what was said in a room an hour ago.

**Mundane examples.** Searching a room, tracking footprints, reading someone's intentions.

#### Passive Reveal
You don't have to roll to notice everything. Your Reveal rank is also your **passive Reveal**. It replaces Passive Perception from *Dungeons & Dragons*.

When something is hidden, the DM gives it a concealment level from 1 to 8. If your Reveal rank equals or beats it, you notice it automatically. If it doesn't, you can still find it by searching, which means rolling for a Reveal spell or mundane action. Something hidden by a spell has a concealment level equal to that spell's level.

\column

| <div style="width:40px">Rank</div> | You notice without trying |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | What's obvious: an open door, a loud noise, someone standing in plain view |
| 2 | What's easy to miss: footprints in mud, a whiff of smoke, a clumsy pickpocket |
| 3 | What's carelessly hidden: a poorly hidden lever, an ambush in tall grass, a nervous liar |
| 4 | What's hidden with care: a trapdoor under a rug, a trained scout, a forged signature |
| 5 | What's expertly hidden: a secret door, a professional assassin, poison in a drink |
| 6 | What few mortals would notice: a disguised shapeshifter, faint traces of magic |
| 7 | What's masterfully hidden: an invisible creature's footsteps, a flaw in an archmage's illusion |
| 8 | What's nearly impossible to see: a god in disguise, a lie told by someone who believes it |

### Create
Create spells make something that stays indefinitely, unless something special makes it disappear. Unlike every other school, a Create spell has no purpose: what you create won't do anything on your behalf, and might have a will of its own. A skeleton summoned with Harm attacks your target and disappears afterwards. The same skeleton made with Create stays, but might attack you, attack your enemy, or simply run away.

The spell's level decides how good your creation is. A level 1 sword is chipped and dull; a level 6 sword rivals the finest blades in the land.

**Spell examples.** A sword, a ray of light, a wooden bridge.

**Mundane examples.** Building a shelter, forging a blade, cooking a meal.

### Passion
When you create your character, you choose a passion. This can be anything, such as the undead, the sea, or music. Any spell that ties into your passion can be cast with Passion. If your passion is the undead and you spell PULL DOWN, skeleton arms might rise from the ground to drag your target down.

Because Passion is so limited, it's also strong. Your Passion rank is always 2 higher than your highest rank in any other school, up to a maximum of 8. In return, your DM will be stricter about whether a Passion spell works, and may ask you to justify it. Why would there be skeletons under your target?

Try not to choose a passion that's too specific, or you'll struggle to use it.

**Mundane examples.** Knowledge and skills related to your passion, such as identifying an old bone or calming a spooked horse.

\page

### Mundane Actions
Mundane actions work like spells, with a few differences:
- **No spelling.** You roll your casting pool for the school as normal, but you don't draw tiles. On your casting turn, you just describe what you do.
- **Mortal limit.** A mundane action can never be higher than level 3, no matter what you roll. Some class features and feats raise this limit.
- **Plausibility.** A mundane action has to be something a skilled person could do with the tools they have. You can't punch a hole through a castle wall, however well you roll.
- **No backfire.** Mundane actions never backfire.

\column

## Rolling the Dice
Almost everything you do in Spellbound starts with the same roll: your **casting pool**. Whether you're casting a fireball or swinging a sword, you roll a handful of dice for the school of magic your action falls under. That one roll tells you three things: whether you succeed, how powerful the action is, and how many letter tiles you get.

### Ranks
Every character has a rank in each school of magic, from 1 to 8. Your rank shows how skilled you are in that school, both with magic and with its mundane actions. You set your starting ranks when you create your character, and raise them as you level up (see *Advancement*).

Your Passion rank works differently: it's always 2 higher than your highest rank in any other school, up to a maximum of 8.

### Casting Pools
Your rank decides which dice you roll. At low ranks you roll more d6s as you improve. From rank 5 onward, your dice get bigger instead.

##### Casting Pools

| Rank | Casting pool |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | 1d6 |
| 2 | 2d6 |
| 3 | 3d6 |
| 4 | 4d6 |
| 5 | 4d8 |
| 6 | 5d8 |
| 7 | 5d10 |
| 8 | 5d12 |

Bigger dice matter for two reasons. They roll 5 or higher more often, which makes your actions stronger, and they can roll higher than 6, which lets you attempt feats that are impossible for less skilled characters.

### Reading Your Roll
When you roll your casting pool, look at it three ways:

1. **Did you succeed?** If at least one die equals or beats the DC, your action succeeds. If none do, it fails.
2. **How strong is it?** Every die that rolled a 5 or higher is called a **surge**. The number of surges is your action's **level**. If you rolled no surges, the level is 1. Your level can never be higher than your rank in that school.
3. **How many tiles do you get?** If you're creating a new spell, add up all your dice and draw that many letter tiles. There's no limit. Mundane actions and saved spells don't draw tiles.

A failed roll gives you nothing: no tiles, no level, and no action this round.

\page

### Difficulty
Your DM sets the difficulty (DC) based on what you're trying to do and who or what you're doing it to.

| <div style="width:40px">DC</div> | <div style="width:60px">Difficulty</div> | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 2 | Trivial | Lighting a candle, opening an unlocked door |
| 3 | Easy | Climbing a rope, calming a dog |
| 4 | Standard | Hitting an ordinary guard, picking a simple lock |
| 5 | Hard | Hitting a trained knight, breaking a warded door |
| 6 | Very hard | Fighting an elite soldier, opening a vault |
| 7–8 | Heroic | Piercing a dragon's hide, breaking a royal ward |
| 9–10 | Legendary | Outwitting an archmage, binding a god's servant |
| 11–12 | Mythic | Facing a demigod, bending the laws of a realm |

Some dice can never roll high enough to succeed at the hardest DCs. A d6 can never roll higher than 6, so a DC of 7 or higher needs at least rank 5 (d8), a DC of 9 or higher needs at least rank 7 (d10), and a DC of 11 or 12 needs rank 8 (d12).

The DM can raise the DC by 1 or 2 if your spell or action is a poor fit for the target, and lower it if you're exploiting a weakness. If you think a DC is unfair, you can always plead your case.

### Adding and Losing Dice
Some features, feats and situations add dice to your casting pool or take them away. An added die is always the same size as the rest of your pool: a rank 5 character who gains a die rolls 5d8 instead of 4d8. Your pool can never drop below one die.

Gaining a die doesn't change your rank, so your level is still capped by your rank.

### Rerolls
Some features let you reroll dice. You always have to keep the new result, even if it's worse. You can't reroll a die more than once.

### Saving Throws
Sometimes something happens to you, and you have to resist it: a trap springs, poison hits your blood, or an enemy's spell grabs hold of you. When that happens, the DM asks for a **saving throw**. Roll the casting pool of the school that fits how you resist. If at least one die equals or beats the DC, you resist.

\column

| <div style="width:60px">School</div> | Resists | Instead of |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Move | Being pushed, grabbed or knocked down; dodging falling rocks, blasts and traps | Strength and Dexterity saves |
| Aid | Poison, disease, exhaustion, extreme heat or cold | Constitution saves |
| Reveal | Illusions, tricks, and things that aren't what they seem | Intelligence saves |
| Control | Charm, fear, and anything that tries to take over your mind | Wisdom and Charisma saves |

If you're using an adventure written for *Dungeons & Dragons*, use the last column to translate its saving throws.

**Against hazards,** the DM sets the DC like any other difficulty. **Against spells,** the DC is the spell's level + 3, so a level 2 spell needs a 5 or higher to resist. This means a spell of level 4 or higher can only be resisted by characters with bigger dice.

A successful saving throw usually means you avoid the effect entirely, or take half damage if it's something you can't fully escape, like an explosion. Damaging spells aimed straight at you don't allow a saving throw unless the DM says otherwise.

### Example
Ash has Move at rank 5 and wants to throw a bandit off a bridge. The DM sets the DC at 6. Ash rolls 4d8 and gets 1, 3, 5 and 8.

- **Success.** The 8 beats the DC of 6, so the action succeeds.
- **Level.** The 5 and 8 are surges, so the action is level 2.
- **Tiles.** Ash is casting a new spell, so they draw 1 + 3 + 5 + 8 = 17 tiles.

On their casting turn, Ash spells WIND GUST and blasts the bandit off the bridge.

## Spellcasting
In Spellbound, you don't pick spells from a list. You make them up by spelling them out with your letter tiles. What a spell does depends on three things: the school you rolled, the word(s) you spelled, and the level you rolled.

### Creating a Spell
On your casting turn, use your tiles to spell out your spell. Then tell the DM what the spell does and how it fits the school you rolled. If the DM approves, the spell takes effect.

A few rules for spelling:
- **Any real word counts.** Nouns, verbs, adjectives, names of places or creatures in your world: if it's a real word, you can use it.
- **As many words as you like.** A spell can be a single word like STING, a phrase like POISON LUNG, or a whole sentence like THE FLOOR TURNS TO ICE, as long as you have the tiles.
- **Blank tiles.** Blank tiles can be used as any letter.
- **No abbreviations or made-up words.** You can only use these when you're bending the rules (see *Bending the Rules*).

The word or words you spell shape the spell, but they don't decide how strong it is. Spelling BECOME GOD at level 1 won't make you a god, but you might get one small divine act. A short spell can be just as powerful as a long one if you roll well.

### Speaking Your Spell
Spells are cast out loud. When your spell takes effect, your character speaks its words, and anyone nearby can hear them.

- **You need your voice.** If you're gagged, magically silenced, or underwater, you can't cast spells, including saved spells. You can still take mundane actions.
- **Spells are heard.** Casting a spell gives away your position if you're hiding, and anyone who hears you knows a spell is coming. This is what lets others counter your spells with the Prepare action.
- **Volume matters.** How loudly you speak decides how far your spell reaches and who can hear it (see *Volume*).

Some class abilities and feats let you cast spells in other ways, such as the Runesmith's Written Word.

#### Volume
When you cast a spell, choose whether to whisper, speak, or shout it. If you don't say, you speak it. The louder you are, the further your spell reaches, but the more people hear it coming.

\column

| <div style="width:60px">Volume</div> | Spell range | Heard within |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Whisper | Touch: something next to you | 5 feet |
| Speak | 60 feet | 60 feet |
| Shout | 120 feet | 300 feet |

Every creature within hearing distance hears your spell. It can counter the spell if it took the Prepare action, and if you were hiding, it knows where you are.

A spell's range is measured in distance, not sound, so a wall between you and your target doesn't stop the spell from reaching it. Whether you can target something you can't see is up to your DM.

#### What Blocks Sound
Obstacles and noise make you harder to hear. Each one makes you one step quieter for anyone on the other side of it: a shout is heard as if you spoke, a spoken spell as if you whispered, and a whisper isn't heard at all.

- **One step quieter.** A closed door, a thin wall, a curtain of falling water, or a very loud place such as a storm or a crowded tavern.
- **Two steps quieter.** A thick stone wall, or being underwater.

These only change who hears your spell, not its range.

A creature that's deafened can't hear any spells, so it can't counter them.

### Justifying Your Spell
The DM approves your spell if it makes sense for what you want to do, and what you want to do fits the school. Spelling STING with Harm to hurt a goblin is easy to approve. Spelling STING with Move to push a boulder will need a good explanation.

If the DM isn't convinced, you can plead your case. If they still say no, the spell doesn't work, but you may keep tiles as if you couldn't spell anything (see *When You Can't Spell*).

{{note
##### Spell Creation
These rules depend heavily on the type of campaign you're playing. More serious campaigns could require spells to fit the theme of the world, not allowing spells like AIOLI MAZE despite it being spelled correctly, while more laid-back campaigns could allow spells incorporating memes or inside jokes.

Remember that, at the end of the day, you're supposed to have fun playing a game with your friends. Bending the rules every now and then isn't so bad.
}}

### Spell Levels
The level you rolled decides how powerful your spell is. If you're unsure whether something is possible at a certain level, ask your DM or plead your case.

| <div style="width:40px">Level</div> | Scale | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Minor magic affecting one creature or object | Tripping a guard, lighting a candle |
| 2 | Several targets, or a stronger effect | Knocking three bandits over |
| 3 | A group, or a notable effect | Filling a courtyard with fog, healing a badly hurt ally |
| 4 | Large scale, with significant impact | Destroying a gate, healing your entire party |
| 5 | A room or a small building | Collapsing a tower floor, calming a riot |
| 6 | Great magic | Creating a huge explosion, moving a building |
| 7 | Legendary magic | Turning a river, holding back a small army |
| 8 | Mythic magic, rarely seen | Raising an island, ending a storm |

For damage and healing, the effect dice already decide how strong a spell is and how many creatures it can reach, so use *Effect Dice* instead of this table.

### Effect Dice
When a spell or mundane action needs a number, such as damage or healing, roll a number of dice equal to its level.

| Action | Effect dice | Several targets |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Harm spell | 1d8 damage per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Aid spell (heal) | Heal 1d8 per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Aid spell (ward) | Absorbs 1d8 damage per level until your next turn | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Passion spell | 1d6 damage or healing per level | Share the dice, at least one each |
| Melee or ranged attack (Harm, mundane) | Your weapon die per level | One target only |
| First aid (Aid, mundane) | Heal 1d4 per level | One target only |

#### Sharing Effect Dice
A spell that damages, heals or creates a ward can affect several creatures by sharing its effect dice between them. Every creature gets at least one die, so a spell can affect as many creatures as its level. Roll each creature's dice separately.

A level 3 Harm spell rolls 3d8. It can hit one goblin for 3d8, two goblins for 2d8 and 1d8, or three goblins for 1d8 each. In the same way, a level 3 ward can be one strong 3d8 ward, or a 1d8 ward on each of three allies.

\column

### Lasting Effects
Some spells don't just happen and end, like a Control spell that holds someone in place.

- **Against unwilling creatures.** Change, Move and Control spells on a creature that doesn't want them last a number of rounds equal to the spell's level. At the end of each of its turns, the creature may try to break free. Player characters make a saving throw against the spell (see *Saving Throws*). Enemies roll their **resist die**, a d6, d8, d10 or d12 depending on how powerful they are, and break free if they roll higher than the spell's level + 2.
- **Outside of combat.** These effects last for the rest of the scene.
- **Reveal spells.** These give you one clear fact or detail per level, or a sense that lasts 10 minutes per level.
- **Create spells.** These are permanent.
- **Anything else.** These effects last until the end of your next turn, unless the DM decides otherwise.

### Creation Quality
Things you make with Create get better and bigger as the spell's level goes up.

| <div style="width:40px">Level</div> | Quality | Largest size |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Crude: a chipped, dull sword | Something you can hold |
| 2 | Serviceable | Something you can carry |
| 3 | Good, well made | Furniture |
| 4 | Fine | A cart |
| 5 | Masterwork | A house |
| 6 | Exceptional | A hall |
| 7 | Legendary | A fortress |
| 8 | Wondrous | A castle |

### Bending the Rules
Sometimes the tiles just won't cooperate. You can misspell a word or bend grammar to make your spell work, such as spelling ISE SPIKE instead of ICE SPIKE. If the DM approves, the spell works as normal, but it backfires.

The DM decides what the backfire is. It's usually related to how you bent the rules: misspelling ICE might give you frostbite. If the DM can't think of anything, you take 1d4 damage per spell level.

### When You Can't Spell
If you can't come up with a spell, you can keep a number of tiles equal to the level you rolled. Choose which ones. You can use them on your next casting turn, together with any new tiles you draw.

\page

### Saving Spells
If you really liked a spell, you can save it. After the spell succeeds, use one of your empty spell slots. From then on, you know that spell at level 1. Most characters start with 2 spell slots and gain more as they level up (see *Advancement*).

To cast a saved spell, choose it during your pre-turn and roll your casting pool for its school as normal. You don't draw tiles. If your roll succeeds, the spell takes effect on your casting turn at its saved level.

Saved spells get stronger the more you use them. Every time you cast one successfully, roll one die of the same size as your casting pool for that school. If the result equals or beats the spell's current level, the spell's level goes up by 1. A saved spell can never be higher than your rank in its school.

During a long rest, you can forget one saved spell to free up its spell slot.

### Example
Rowan has Harm at rank 3. During her pre-turn, she attacks a group of three cultists. The DM sets the DC at 4, and Rowan rolls 3d6: 2, 5 and 6. She succeeds, rolls two surges, and draws 13 tiles.

On her casting turn, she wants to hit as many cultists as she can. She doesn't have the letters for FIREBALL, but she can spell EMBER RAIN. The DM approves. The spell is level 2, so it rolls 2d8, which she can share between up to two creatures: she gives one die to each of two cultists, and each takes 1d8 damage.

Rowan likes the spell, so she uses a spell slot to save EMBER RAIN at level 1. The next time she casts it successfully, she rolls a d6 to see if it grows. She rolls a 3, which beats its level of 1, so EMBER RAIN is now a level 2 saved spell.

\column

## Health and Resting
Adventuring is dangerous. This chapter covers how your character gets hurt, how they recover, and what happens when things go badly wrong.

### Hit Points
Your **hit points** show how much punishment you can take before you fall. Your maximum hit points come from your class (see *Character Creation* and *Advancement*). When you take damage, subtract it from your current hit points. When you're healed, add it back, up to your maximum.

### Armor and Shields
Armor reduces every instance of damage you take by its armor value, to a minimum of 1 damage. A goblin's spear that deals 5 damage only deals 3 to someone in medium armor.

A **shield** adds 1 to your armor value, but it takes up one hand. Only classes and feats that say so let you use a shield. You can't hold a two-handed weapon while using one, and you'll need your other hand free for things like a Runesmith's Written Word.

### Wards
Some Aid spells create a **ward** that absorbs damage. Damage hits the ward first, and anything left over goes through to you. A ward lasts until your next turn, or until it has absorbed as much damage as it can. If you have more than one ward, only the strongest one counts.

### Resting
Adventurers need to rest to recover.

- **Short rest.** At least an hour of downtime: eating, bandaging wounds, catching your breath. You can take up to two short rests a day. At the end of a short rest, roll a number of your class's hit dice equal to half your level, rounded up, and heal that many hit points.
- **Long rest.** At least 8 hours, most of it sleeping. You can take one long rest a day. At the end of a long rest, you regain all your hit points, all uses of your signature ability, and every feature that works "once per long rest".

### Once Per…
Many features can only be used a limited number of times.

- **Once per round** resets at the start of every round of combat.
- **Once per combat** resets when a combat ends.
- **Once per long rest** resets when you finish a long rest.
- **Once per session** resets at the start of every play session, however much time has passed in the story.

\page

### Dropping to 0 Hit Points
When you drop to 0 hit points, you're **downed**. You fall unconscious, you can't take pre-turns or casting turns, and you can't speak, so you can't cast spells.

At the start of every round while you're downed, make a **death roll**: roll a d20.

- **10 or higher.** You gain a success.
- **9 or lower.** You gain a failure.
- **A natural 1.** You gain two failures.
- **A natural 20.** You wake up with 1 hit point.

Taking damage while you're downed also gives you a failure. When you have three successes, you're stable: you stay downed, but stop rolling, and wake up with 1 hit point after an hour. When you have three failures, your character dies.

Any healing, from an Aid spell, first aid or a potion, wakes you up immediately with that many hit points and clears all your successes and failures. An ally can also **stabilize** you with a successful mundane Aid action, just as if you'd rolled three successes.

{{note
##### Lasting Scars
Not every table wants characters to die. If your group prefers, a character with three failures survives instead, but gains a lasting scar chosen by the DM: a limp, a missing finger, a voice that no longer carries when they shout. This fits Spellbound especially well when the scar affects how they cast.
}}

### Conditions
Some effects leave you in a particular state. These are the conditions Spellbound uses most. For any others, such as poisoned or frightened, use the rules from *Dungeons & Dragons*.

- **Deafened.** You can't hear. You can't counter spells you can't hear, and you can't benefit from songs like a Loresinger's Battle Hymn.
- **Silenced.** You can't make a sound. You can't cast spoken spells, but you can still take mundane actions, and write spells if you're able to.
- **Hidden.** No one knows exactly where you are. Casting a spell out loud gives away your position to everyone who hears it (see *Volume*).
- **Distracted.** Your attention is elsewhere, usually because you're fighting someone else. A Trickster's Ambush works against distracted creatures.
- **Downed.** You're at 0 hit points and unconscious (see *Dropping to 0 Hit Points*).

\column

### Example
Bram, a level 3 Warden in heavy armor, is hit by an ogre's club for 11 damage. His armor reduces it to 8, which takes him from 6 hit points to 0, and he's downed.

At the start of the next round, he makes a death roll and gets a 7: his first failure. Before his next roll, his friend Mira casts MEND with Aid at level 1 and heals him for 1d8, rolling a 5. Bram wakes up with 5 hit points, and his failure is cleared.

\page

## Character Creation
Creating a character in Spellbound is quicker than in *Dungeons & Dragons*. There are no ability scores to roll: everything your character can do comes from their ranks in the schools of magic, their class, and their feats.

### 1. Choose a Class
Your class decides how tough you are, what you can wear and wield, and which features you gain as you level up. Every class can cast spells, but some lean more on magic and others on mundane actions. Each class also has a **signature ability** that only it can use. Each class has its own chapter with its full features.

| Class | Role |
|:--- |:--- |
| Wordweaver | Pure caster | 
| Loresinger | Support | 
| Runesmith | Crafter | 
| Trickster | Skirmisher | 
| Spellblade | Warrior-mage | 
| Warden | Protector | 

### 2. Set Your Ranks
All seven schools (every school except Passion) start at rank 1. You then have 10 points to distribute among them as you like, adding 1 rank per point. After that, add your class's 3 bonus ranks.

No school can be higher than rank 4 at level 1, including after your class bonus.

{{note
##### Spreading Your Points
A character with a few high ranks is powerful in their specialty, but struggles outside it. A character with many medium ranks can try anything, but rarely casts high-level spells. Remember that your highest rank also sets your Passion rank, Move sets your initiative, Reveal sets your passive Reveal, and Move, Aid, Reveal and Control cover your saving throws.
}}

### 3. Choose Your Passion
Pick something your character truly cares about, such as the sea, music, the undead, or clockwork. Your Passion rank is your highest other rank + 2, so most characters start with a Passion rank of 6. See *Passion* for advice on choosing one.

### 4. Choose a Feat
Pick one feat, either from the general feats or from your class's own list. See *Feats*.

### 5. Determine Your Hit Points
At level 1, your maximum hit points are the highest number on your class's hit die + 6. A Warden starts with 18 hit points, while a Wordweaver starts with 12.

\column

### 6. Choose Your Equipment
Choose one weapon and one type of armor that your class allows, plus ordinary adventuring gear like a backpack, rope, and rations. Your DM may let you start with more.

**Weapons.** When you attack with a weapon as a mundane action, you roll its weapon die once per level.

| Type | Weapon die | Examples | 
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Light | d4 | Dagger, sling | 
| Simple | d6 | Club, spear, shortbow | 
| Martial | d8 | Longsword, longbow | 
| Heavy | d10 | Greataxe, maul | 

**Armor.** Armor reduces every instance of damage you take by its armor value, to a minimum of 1 damage.

| Type | Armor value | Examples |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| None | 0 | Robes, everyday clothes |
| Light | 1 | Leather, padded cloth |
| Medium | 2 | Chain shirt, scale |
| Heavy | 3 | Chain mail, plate |
| Shield | +1 | Wooden shield, kite shield |

### 7. Note Your Spell Slots
You start with 2 empty spell slots (Wordweavers start with 3). You fill them by saving spells you've cast during play. See *Saving Spells*.

### 8. Fill in the Details
Finally, decide who your character is: their name, where they come from, what they look like, and what drives them.

You can choose a species and background from *Dungeons & Dragons* to help shape your character. Keep any traits that don't depend on ability scores or skills, such as darkvision, and ignore ability score increases and skill proficiencies, since Spellbound has neither. A background is also a great way to explain your passion: a former sailor might well be passionate about the sea.

### Starting at a Higher Level
For a oneshot, your DM might ask you to start at level 3 so everyone has a few more features to play with. See *Advancement*.

\page

### Example
Tamsin is making a Wordweaver. She wants a character who's good at fighting, but can also solve problems in clever ways.

**Ranks.** All her schools start at rank 1. She spends her 10 points: 2 on Harm, 2 on Move, 2 on Reveal, and 1 each on Aid, Change, Control and Create. Her Wordweaver bonus adds 1 more to Harm, Change and Create.

| School | Rank | Casting pool |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Harm | 4 | 4d6 |
| Aid | 2 | 2d6 |
| Change | 3 | 3d6 |
| Move | 3 | 3d6 |
| Control | 2 | 2d6 |
| Reveal | 3 | 3d6 |
| Create | 3 | 3d6 |
| Passion (storms) | 6 | 5d8 |

**Passion.** Tamsin grew up on a stormy coast, so she picks storms. Her highest rank is 4, so her Passion rank is 6.

**Feat, hit points and equipment.** She picks the Cantrips feat. As a Wordweaver, she has a d6 hit die, so she starts with 12 hit points. She takes a dagger (d4), wears no armor, and has 3 empty spell slots.

**Everything else.** She adds +3 to initiative from her Move rank, and her passive Reveal is 3, so she notices carelessly hidden things without trying.

## Wordweaver
Wordweavers are scholars of language who believe every word holds power. Where others see a pile of letters, a Wordweaver sees a dozen spells waiting to be spoken. They have the most ranks, the most saved spells, and the best control over their tiles, but they rely almost entirely on magic.

##### The Wordweaver

| Level | Features | Torrent of Words |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 1 | Torrent of Words, Tile Craft, Deep Study | 2 |
| 2 | Subclass feature | 2 |
| 3 | Overchannel | 2 |
| 4 | — | 2 |

| 7 | — | 3 |

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 12 hit points (6 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d6 or take 4, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can't wear armor. You can use light weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 3 ranks to any schools of your choice.

### Torrent of Words
Words pour out of you faster than you can think them. During your pre-turn, you can start a Torrent of Words without using your action. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed.

While it lasts, every die in your casting pool that rolls a 4 or higher counts as a surge, instead of 5 or higher. Your level is still capped by your rank.

You can use Torrent of Words a number of times shown in the Wordweaver table, and regain all uses after a long rest.

### Level 1: Tile Craft
Once per round, after your pre-turn, you can swap up to 2 of your tiles for random tiles from the pile.

### Level 1: Deep Study
You have one extra spell slot.

### Level 3: Overchannel
Once per round, after a successful pre-turn, you can raise your spell's level by 1, up to your rank. You take 1d8 damage when the spell takes effect.

\column

### Level 6: Blended Casting
During your pre-turn, you can name two schools and roll the casting pool of the lower-ranked one. If your spell fits both purposes, it does both, such as a blast that harms a foe and hurls them back.

### Level 9: Echo
Once per combat, after casting a spell, you can skip your next pre-turn and cast the same spell again at the same level on your next casting turn.

### Level 12: Words of Making
Once per session, a spell you cast takes effect at level 8, however many surges you rolled.

### Wordweaver Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Word Hoard.** Tile Craft lets you swap up to 4 tiles.
- **Arcane Recovery.** Once per long rest, after a short rest, roll to grow one of your saved spells as if you'd just cast it.
- **Long Words (4+).** A spell of 8 or more letters takes effect 1 level higher, up to your rank.
- **Sculpt Spell (4+).** Your spells that affect an area can leave out any creatures you choose.
- **Multicast (8+).** If your tiles spell two separate spells, you can cast both on one casting turn. Each takes effect 1 level lower, to a minimum of 1.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Lexicographer
Lexicographers collect words the way others collect coins. The longer and more precise the word, the stronger their magic.

- **Level 2: Well-Read.** Whenever you draw tiles for a new spell, draw 2 extra tiles, then return any 2 tiles to the pile.
- **Level 5: Precise Wording.** If your spell has 7 or more letters, its effect dice are one size bigger: d6s become d8s, and d8s become d10s.
- **Level 10: Unabridged.** Once per combat, if your spell uses every tile you have, with at least 6 tiles, it takes effect 2 levels higher, up to your rank.

\page

#### Devotee
Devotees give themselves over to what they love. Their passion burns brighter than any other caster's, and some even find room in their hearts for a second one.

- **Level 2: Second Passion.** Choose a second passion. You can cast Passion spells that tie into either of your passions, using your normal Passion rank.
- **Level 5: Burning Passion.** Your Passion spells roll d8s for their effect dice instead of d6s.
- **Level 10: Kindred Passions.** Once per combat, a Passion spell that ties into both of your passions takes effect 2 levels higher, up to level 8.

#### Archivist
Archivists value a reliable spell over a clever one. Their saved spells grow faster and stronger than anyone else's.

- **Level 2: Catalogue.** You gain 2 extra spell slots. When you roll to grow a saved spell, roll twice and use the higher result.
- **Level 5: Annotated Spells.** When you save a spell, it starts at level 2 instead of 1, up to your rank.
- **Level 10: Grand Library.** Once per combat, you can cast two of your saved spells on the same casting turn. Roll your pre-turn for the lower-ranked school.

##### Quick Build
Want to start playing right away? Use this build for a level 1 Wordweaver: a storm-touched scholar who solves fights with big words and problems with clever ones.

- **Ranks.** Harm 4, Change 3, Reveal 3, Move 3, Aid 3, Control 2, Create 2. Your Wordweaver bonus goes to Harm, Change and Reveal.
- **Passion.** Storms, at rank 6.
- **Feat.** Cantrips, for small extra spells every turn.
- **Equipment.** A dagger (d4) and no armor.
- **At a glance.** 12 hit points, armor 0, initiative +3, passive Reveal 3, and 3 spell slots.

## Loresinger
Loresingers are bards, storytellers and keepers of old songs. Their magic is at its best when it's shared, and a Loresinger in the party makes everyone around them stronger. They're at home both in battle and at the negotiating table.

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light and simple weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 1 rank to Aid, 1 to Control, and 1 to any school of your choice.

**Silver tongue.** Your mundane Control actions, such as persuading or deceiving someone, have a mortal limit of 4.

### Battle Hymn
You start a song that carries your allies along. During your pre-turn, you can start a Battle Hymn without using your action. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed or silenced.

While it lasts, every ally who can hear you adds one die to their casting pool. Singing doesn't use up your actions, and you can still speak your spells between verses.

During the pre-turn phase, you can give up to 3 of your tiles to allies.

### Level 1: Inspiring Word
Once per round, you can let an ally reroll one die of their casting pool.

Anyone you heal or protect with Aid, whether with a spell or a mundane action, adds one die to their next casting pool.

### Level 12: Anthem
Once per session, every ally rolls their next pre-turn as if their rank were 1 higher, up to rank 8.

### Loresinger Feats

- **Mocking Verse.** When your Control spell or mundane Control action succeeds, the target rolls one die fewer in its next casting pool.
- **Word Bank (4+).** Shared Verse lets you give up to 6 tiles, to any ally who can hear you.
- **Chorus Line (4+).** You can use Inspiring Word twice per round.
- **Encore (8+).** You can use Countersong twice per combat.

### Subclasses

#### Skald
Skalds sing on the front line, where their songs are loudest. They fight alongside the allies they inspire.

- **Level 2: War Chant.** You can wear medium armor and use martial weapons. While Battle Hymn lasts, your allies' weapon attacks deal an extra 1d4 damage.
- **Level 5: Battle Rhythm.** While Battle Hymn lasts, your weapon attacks have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to 5.
- **Level 10: Saga of Heroes.** Once per combat, when an ally who can hear you drops to 0 hit points, they drop to 1 instead.

#### Keeper of Lore
Keepers of Lore remember every story ever told to them, and know exactly which word their friends need next.

- **Level 2: Well of Stories.** Your mundane Reveal actions have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to 5. Once per long rest, you can ask the DM two questions about a creature, place or object, and get honest answers.
- **Level 5: Prompter.** Once per round, after an ally reveals their spell, you can give them up to 2 of your tiles to change or extend their spell before it takes effect.
- **Level 10: Rewrite the Tale.** Once per session, after any creature you can hear rolls its casting pool, you can make it reroll the whole pool.

#### Siren
Sirens sing songs that pull enemies off course. Their magic charms, lulls and commands.

- **Level 2: Enchanting Voice.** Enemies trying to break free from your Control spells must roll higher than the spell's level + 3, instead of + 2.
- **Level 5: Siren Song.** When you start a Battle Hymn, you can sing a siren song instead. While it lasts, every enemy who can hear you rolls one die fewer in its casting pool, instead of your allies adding one.
- **Level 10: Voice of Command.** Once per session, a Control spell you cast affects every enemy who can hear you.

## Runesmith
Runesmiths are artificers who believe a word is worth more written down than spoken aloud. They carve spells into stone, steel and parchment, build tireless constructs, and make things that last. In a fight, they're slower than other casters, but their spells can't be heard coming.

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light and simple weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 2 ranks to Create and 1 to Change.

**Handy.** Your mundane Change and Create actions, such as picking a lock or forging a blade, have a mortal limit of 4.

### Written Word
You can cast spells by writing them instead of speaking them. Whenever you cast a spell, you can choose to write it.

- **What you need.** A free hand and something to write on. A quill and parchment works, but so does a finger in the dust or a knife on a tabletop.
- **Silent.** A written spell makes no sound. It doesn't give away your position, can't be countered with the Prepare action, and works while you're silenced or gagged. Its range is the same as if it was spoken.
- **Slower.** Writing takes longer than speaking. A written spell takes effect at the end of the round, after every casting turn.

### Level 1: Inscribe
During a short or long rest, you can roll a pre-turn in any school and spell a spell as normal, but store it in an object as a rune instead of casting it. Anyone holding the object can release the spell without using up their action, after which the rune fades. Runes hold spells up to level 2. You can have 1 rune at a time, plus 1 more for every three levels you have.

### Level 1: Tinker's Eye
Things you make with Create are one quality step better than their spell level, though not any bigger.

### Level 3: Construct
During a long rest, you can build a construct companion. It has 4 hit points per level you have and 1 armor. On your casting turn, it can make a weapon attack with a casting pool of rank 2 and a d6 weapon die. If it's destroyed, you can build a new one during your next long rest.

### Level 6: Deep Runes
Your runes can hold spells up to level 4. Your construct now attacks with a casting pool of rank 3 and a d8 weapon die.

### Level 9: Masterwork
Once per long rest, something you make with Create or a mundane Create action gains one lasting property from a school you have at rank 3 or higher, such as a blade that burns for an extra 1d6 damage. You can have 3 such items at once.

### Level 12: Grand Engine
Your runes can hold spells up to your rank. Once per session, you can release all your runes on a single casting turn.

### Runesmith Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

- **Quick Inscribe.** In combat, you can store a spell you just spelled as a rune instead of casting it.
- **Improvised Tools.** Your mundane Change and Create actions don't need proper tools, and take half the usual time.
- **Rune Ammunition (4+).** A rune on a weapon is released for free when your weapon attack hits.
- **Reinforced Construct (4+).** Your construct has twice as many hit points and 2 armor.
- **Living Workshop (8+).** You can have 2 more runes at once, and your runes recharge after a long rest instead of fading when used.

#### Glyphwarden

## Trickster
Tricksters are thieves, spies and con artists who get by on quick fingers and quicker wits. They steal letters from their enemies' hands, strike when no one's looking, and twist other casters' words against them. A Trickster is never quite where you think they are, and neither is their voice.

##### The Trickster

| Level | Features | Thrown Voice |

### Class Features
**Hit points.** You start with 14 hit points (8 + 6). Each time you level up, roll a d8 or take 5, and add it to your maximum hit points.

**Armor and weapons.** You can wear light armor. You can use light, simple and martial weapons.

**Bonus ranks.** Add 1 rank to Harm, 1 to Change, and 1 to Reveal.

### Thrown Voice
You can make your voice come from anywhere. During your pre-turn, you can start Thrown Voice without using your action. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed.

While it lasts, your spells sound like they come from any point you can see. Casting doesn't give away your position, enemies can't tell who cast your spells, and they can only counter them if they can see you. Your Ambush also works against any target, even one that isn't distracted.

Once per round, when your weapon attack or Harm spell damages a target that's unaware of you, distracted, or fighting one of your allies, it deals an extra 2d6 damage. This becomes 3d6 at level 6 and 4d6 at level 9.

Choose two schools other than Passion. Your mundane actions in those schools have a mortal limit of 4. At level 6, this becomes 5.

### Level 3: Slippery
You take half damage from spells that affect an area. After your casting turn, you can slip away from anyone next to you without them getting a chance to stop you.

Once per combat, when an enemy reveals its spell, you can swap one of its tiles for a random tile from the pile. The enemy must use the new tiles, bend the rules and take a backfire, or let the spell drop to level 1.

### Level 9: Vanish
Once per combat, you can disappear from sight until the end of your next casting turn. If you Ambush during that time, roll twice as many dice for its extra damage.

### Level 12: Perfect Heist
Once per session, when an enemy casts a spell, you can save it into one of your empty spell slots at its current level, up to your rank in its school.

- **Light Fingers.** Sleight lets you take 2 tiles.

- **Dirty Fighting (4+).** When your weapon attack hits, you can also trip, blind or disarm your target. It rolls one die fewer in its next casting pool.
- **Uncanny Dodge (4+).** Once per round, you can halve the damage from one hit.
- **Master Thief (8+).** You can use Twist the Word twice per combat.

#### Shadow
Shadows are assassins and scouts. By the time anyone notices them, the fight is usually over.

- **Level 10: House Always Wins.** Once per combat, after an enemy rolls its casting pool, roll one die of the same size. You can replace any one of its dice with your roll.

Magic floods your blade and your body. During your pre-turn, you can enter a Spellfury without using your action. It lasts for 3 rounds, and ends early if you're downed.

Your weapon attacks have a mortal limit of 4.

Your Bladewords have no letter limit, and take effect at the same level as your weapon attack.

Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of these instead of a general feat. Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require that character level.

### Subclasses
At level 2, choose a subclass. It shapes how you play your class, and gives you features at levels 2, 5 and 10.

#### Heartblade
Heartblades fight with their passion in their hands. Their weapon takes on the shape of what they love most: a cutlass for a lover of the sea, a bone blade for someone fascinated by the dead.

- **Level 5: Heartfelt Bladewords.** Bladewords that tie into your passion take effect 1 level higher.

Spellguards use their blade as a shield against magic, turning enemy spells aside and stealing their power.

## Feats
Feats are special talents that set your character apart: a knack for persuasion, a voice that shakes the walls, or a way with letters that other casters envy.

### Gaining Feats
You gain one feat at level 1, and another every time you level up. At levels 4, 8 and 12, you gain two feats instead of one. By level 12, you'll have 15 feats.

### Choosing Feats
Whenever you gain a feat, you can choose one of the general feats in this chapter, or one of the feats from your class's chapter.

- **Level requirements.** Feats marked 4+ or 8+ require you to be at least that level.
- **Once only.** You can only take each feat once, unless it says otherwise.
- **Specific beats general.** If a feat or class feature contradicts a general rule, the feat or feature wins. If two of them contradict each other, the DM decides.

### Body and Mind Feats
- **Alert.** Add 5 to your initiative rolls. You can't be caught unaware, so a Trickster's Ambush only works on you if you're distracted or fighting one of their allies.
- **Lucky.** Once per session, you can reroll your whole casting pool.
- **Martial Training.** You can use martial weapons and wear medium armor.
- **Shield Adept.** You can use shields.
- **Tough.** Your maximum hit points increase by 2 for every level you have, and by 2 more every time you level up. You can take this feat twice.
- **Steady Mind (4+).** Add one die to your casting pool when you make a saving throw.

### Skill Feats
- **Trained.** Choose a school other than Passion. Your mundane actions in that school have a mortal limit 1 higher, up to a maximum of 5. You can take this feat several times, for different schools or the same one.
- **Persuasive.** Add one die to your casting pool for mundane Control actions, such as persuading, deceiving or intimidating someone.
- **Keen Senses.** Add one die to your casting pool for mundane Reveal actions, such as searching a room or tracking someone.
- **Medic.** When you take a short rest, you and every ally resting with you heal extra hit points equal to your level. Your first aid can also cure poison and disease.

\column

### Spellcraft Feats
- **Extra Slot.** You gain one extra spell slot. You can take this feat several times.
- **Polyglot.** Words in any language your character speaks count as correctly spelled.
- **Signature Spell (4+).** Choose one of your saved spells. Once per combat, you can cast it at its saved level without rolling during your pre-turn. If you forget the spell, you can choose a new one after a long rest.
- **Spell Focus (4+).** Choose Harm, Aid or Passion. Spells of that school roll effect dice one size bigger: d6s become d8s, and d8s become d10s. You can take this feat several times, for different schools.
- **Twin Spell (8+).** Once per combat, a spell that targets one creature can target a second creature as well.
- **Reserve of Power (8+).** Once per combat, you can take a second pre-turn and a second casting turn in the same round, right after your first.
- **Living Legend (8+).** Once per session, after rolling your casting pool, you can turn one die to its highest number.

### Tile Feats
- **Cantrips.** As a bonus action, you can spell a word of 3 letters or fewer with your leftover tiles. It takes effect at level 1, in any school except Harm and Passion.
- **Letter Hoard.** At the end of every casting turn, you can keep up to 3 unused tiles, or more if you couldn't spell anything.
- **Wild Letters (4+).** Once per combat, roll a d6 and replace that many of your tiles with blank tiles.

### Voice Feats
- **Booming Voice.** When you shout a spell, every enemy within 10 feet of you is deafened until the end of your next casting turn. Deafened creatures can't hear spells, so they can't counter them.
- **Carrying Voice.** Obstacles and noise never make you quieter for creatures you choose. Your allies can always hear you, even through a closed door or over a storm.
- **Soft-Spoken.** Your whispered spells reach up to 30 feet, but can still only be heard within 5 feet of you.
- **Lip Reader.** You can counter spells cast by creatures you can see, even if you can't hear them.
- **Commanding Voice (4+).** When you shout a Control spell, it can affect one extra creature within range, without needing a higher level.
- **Silent Casting (8+).** Once per combat, you can cast a spell by only mouthing the words. It reaches as far as a spoken spell, but makes no sound, so it can't be heard or countered. It takes effect 1 level lower, to a minimum of 1.

## Advancement
As your character adventures, they grow more skilled and more powerful. This is shown by their **level**, which goes from 1 to 12.

### Gaining Levels
Spellbound uses milestones: you level up when your DM says so, usually after an important moment in the story, such as clearing a dungeon, defeating a villain, or completing a quest. You don't track experience points.

## Running Enemies
*This chapter is for the DM.*

Enemies follow the same rules as player characters. They take pre-turns and casting turns, roll casting pools, count surges, and spell their spells out loud, where the players can hear and counter them. This chapter shows how to build enemies quickly and keep fights fair as the party grows.

### Party Level
Enemies are built around the **party level (PL)**: the average level of the player characters, rounded down. A party of three level 3 characters and one level 5 has a party level of 3.

### Tiers
Every enemy belongs to one of four tiers. Use the table to find its numbers.

##### Enemy Stats

| Tier | Hit points | Armor | Resist die | Weapon die |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Minion | 3 + 2×PL | 0 | d6 | d6 |
| Standard | 8 + 5×PL | 0–1 | d6 | d8 |
| Elite | 15 + 8×PL | 1–2 | d8 | d10 |
| Boss | 30 + 15×PL | 2 | d10 (d12 if legendary) | d12 |

##### Best Rank and Guard DC

| Tier | PL 1–4 | PL 5–8 | PL 9–12 |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Minion | Rank 2, DC 3 | Rank 3, DC 3 | Rank 4, DC 4 |
| Standard | Rank 2, DC 4 | Rank 4, DC 5 | Rank 5, DC 6 |
| Elite | Rank 3, DC 5 | Rank 5, DC 6 | Rank 6, DC 7 |
| Boss | Rank 4, DC 6 | Rank 6, DC 7 | Rank 8, DC 8 |

- **Best rank** is the rank of the school the enemy is best at. Its other useful schools are 1 or 2 ranks lower, and everything else is rank 1.
- **Guard DC** is the default difficulty for spells and mundane actions aimed at the enemy. Raise it by 1 or 2 for a spell that suits the target poorly, and lower it for one that exploits a weakness.
- **Resist die** is what the enemy rolls to break free of Change, Move and Control effects (see *Lasting Effects*), and to resist hazards. Against a hazard, it resists if it equals or beats the DC.
- **Weapon die** is the die its weapon attacks roll, once per level.

\column

### Enemy Actions
- **Mortal limit.** Enemy mundane actions have a mortal limit of 3 for minions and standard enemies, 4 for elites, and 5 for bosses. A dragon's bite is still a mundane action: it's what a dragon does without magic.
- **Bosses act twice.** A boss takes two pre-turns and two casting turns every round, at two different points in the initiative order. Roll initiative for it twice.
- **Signature tricks.** Give elites and bosses one special ability, much like a class's signature ability. An ogre might stamp the ground and knock everyone near it prone, or a lich might drain a tile from every player who hears it.

### Enemy Spells
Spelling for every enemy slows the game down. Instead, give each enemy that casts spells 1 to 3 **known spells**, like a player's saved spells: a goblin shaman's STING, a cultist's CURSE, a lich's WITHER. Known spells start at level 1 or 2, and up to the enemy's rank for elites and bosses.

Enemies cast known spells the same way players cast saved spells: they roll their pool during their pre-turn, draw no tiles, and cast at the known spell's level.

Save real spelling for bosses, where the drama is worth it. Draw tiles for a boss like any player, and spell its words aloud at the table. Players love countering a villain's spell, and love it even more when a Trickster twists the word into nonsense.

Enemies speak their spells, so the rules for *Volume* and *What Blocks Sound* apply to them too. A cultist whispering behind a door can't be countered by anyone who can't hear them.

### Building Encounters
A fair fight costs one point of **budget** per player character.

| Tier | Cost |
|:--- |:--- |
| Minion | ½ |
| Standard | 1 |
| Elite | 2 |
| Boss | 4 |

- **Fair fight.** 1 point per player.
- **Hard fight.** 1.5 points per player.
- **Deadly fight.** More than 2 points per player. Someone will probably go down, and might not get back up.

A party of four facing a fair fight might meet four standard enemies, or one elite and four minions. A boss fight for the same party might be one boss with two minions.

\page

### Using *Dungeons & Dragons* Monsters
You can use monsters from *Dungeons & Dragons* adventures by translating them:

1. **Tier.** Pick the tier that matches the monster's role: weak swarming creatures are minions, ordinary foes are standard, tough foes are elites, and the big villain is a boss.
2. **Numbers.** Use the tier table for hit points, armor, ranks, Guard DC, resist die and weapon die. Don't convert the original numbers.
3. **Attacks.** Each attack becomes a mundane Harm action with the tier's weapon die.
4. **Spells and abilities.** Turn the monster's spells into known spells, and its most memorable ability into a signature trick.

## Judging Spells
*This chapter is for the DM.*

In Spellbound, every spell is a small negotiation between the player and the DM. This chapter helps you make those calls quickly and fairly, so the game keeps moving.

### Three Questions
When a player reveals a spell, ask yourself three things:

1. **Are they real words?** Every word in the spell has to be real, or the player has to accept a backfire for bending the rules.
2. **Do the words fit the goal?** The spell should make sense for what the player wants to do. FREEZE fits stopping a guard in his tracks. CHEESE probably doesn't, unless the player has a very good story.
3. **Does the goal fit the school and level?** The goal has to match the school they rolled, and its size has to match the level (see *Spell Levels*).

If all three are a yes, the spell works. Don't spend long on it: a quick yes keeps the table excited.

### Generous Words, Strict Levels
Be generous with words and strict with levels. Players should feel free to be creative with what they spell, because the level already limits how powerful it can be. A player who spells DEATH at level 1 doesn't kill anything, but you can let them deal 1d8 damage with a chill that feels like death.

If a spell is a stretch but the player's explanation is fun, you can let the spell work at 1 level lower instead of saying no.

### Common Rulings
- **Word forms.** Plurals, verb tenses and other forms of a word all count. FROZE is as good as FREEZE.
- **Names.** Names of people, places and creatures count if they exist in your world. The player can't just invent one.
- **Other languages.** Words from other real languages count as bending the rules, unless the character has the Polyglot feat.
- **Killing words.** Words like DIE or KILL never kill outright. They deal damage like any Harm spell.
- **The same spell again.** Players can spell the same spell every turn if they draw the letters. If one spell starts to dominate, remind them they could save it in one of their spell slots instead.
- **Two schools at once.** If a spell seems to fit two schools, it only does what the school they rolled allows. A FIREBALL rolled with Move might knock a goblin back, but it won't burn it.

\column

### Judging Passion
Passion is powerful, so it should always need a reason. Before you approve a Passion spell, ask the player why their passion would be here. An undead passion in a graveyard is easy. An undead passion in the middle of the sea needs a sunken shipwreck, or a drowned sailor, or something just as good.

Reward players who plan ahead. A character passionate about music who carries a lute is easier to approve than one who doesn't.

### Backfires
When a player bends the rules, choose a backfire that fits how they bent them, or roll on this table.

| d8 | Backfire |
|:--- |:--- |
| 1 | **Recoil.** You take 1d4 damage per spell level. |
| 2 | **Echo.** The spell also affects you, at half strength. |
| 3 | **Tongue-tied.** You roll one die fewer in your next casting pool. |
| 4 | **Wild aim.** The spell hits a random creature within range instead. |
| 5 | **Too loud.** Everyone within 300 feet hears the spell, as if you'd shouted it. |
| 6 | **Drained.** You draw 3 fewer tiles the next time you draw. |
| 7 | **Twisted.** The spell works, but in a strange way the DM chooses, inspired by the misspelling. |
| 8 | **Odd side effect.** The spell works, and something harmless but strange happens: your hair turns blue, or everything smells of onions for an hour. |

### Keeping the Game Moving
- **Spell during pre-turns.** Remind players to work on their words while others take their pre-turns, not when their casting turn comes up.
- **Use a timer.** If casting turns drag, give each player a minute to reveal their spell. When time runs out, they keep tiles as if they couldn't spell.
- **Let the table help.** Other players can suggest words, but the caster decides. Helping each other spell is part of the fun.
- **Don't punish clever spelling.** If a player finds a surprising word or phrase that fits perfectly, let it shine. That moment is what Spellbound is about.

### Example
Rowan rolls Harm at level 2 and spells ISE SPIKE, misspelling ICE. The word is bent, the goal fits Harm, and a level 2 spell has two dice to share, enough to hit two cultists. The DM approves the spell, so each cultist takes 1d8 damage. For the backfire, the DM picks one that fits the bent word: frost creeps up Rowan's fingers, and she takes 2d4 damage.

## Appendix A: Quick Reference

### The Core Loop
1. **Pre-turn.** Name a school, and whether you cast a new spell, a saved spell, or take a mundane action. Get a DC and roll your casting pool.
2. **Read the roll.** Succeed if any die equals or beats the DC. Count surges (dice showing 5+) for your level. For a new spell, draw tiles equal to the total.
3. **Casting turn.** Spell it, bend it, keep tiles if you can't spell, or describe your mundane action. Move and take a bonus action if something allows it.

### Casting Pools

| Rank | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Pool | 1d6 | 2d6 | 3d6 | 4d6 | 4d8 | 5d8 | 5d10 | 5d12 |

### Difficulty

| DC | Difficulty | Lowest rank |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| 2–6 | Trivial to very hard | 1 |
| 7–8 | Heroic | 5 |
| 9–10 | Legendary | 7 |
| 11–12 | Mythic | 8 |

### Effect Dice

| Action | Effect dice | Several targets |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Harm spell | 1d8 per level | Share, at least one die each |
| Aid spell (heal) | 1d8 per level | Share, at least one die each |
| Aid spell (ward) | Absorbs 1d8 per level until your next turn | Share, at least one die each |
| Passion spell | 1d6 per level | Share, at least one die each |
| Weapon attack | Weapon die per level | — |
| First aid | 1d4 per level | — |

A spell can affect as many creatures as it has effect dice.

### Mundane Actions
No tiles, no backfire, and a mortal limit of level 3 unless a feature raises it.

### Volume

| Volume | Range | Heard within |
|:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Whisper | Touch | 5 feet |
| Speak | 60 feet | 60 feet |
| Shout | 120 feet | 300 feet |

A door or thin wall makes you one step quieter. A thick stone wall makes you two steps quieter.

### Saving Throws

| School | Resists |
|:--- |:--- |
| Move | Being pushed, grabbed or knocked down; dodging |
| Aid | Poison, disease, exhaustion, heat and cold |
| Reveal | Illusions and tricks |
| Control | Charm, fear and mind control |

Against a spell, the DC is its level + 3.

### Lasting Effects
Change, Move and Control effects on an unwilling creature last 1 round per level. The target tries to break free at the end of each of its turns.

### Death Rolls
At 0 hit points, roll a d20 at the start of every round. 10+ is a success, 9 or lower a failure. A natural 1 is two failures, and a natural 20 wakes you with 1 hit point. Three successes and you're stable, three failures and you die.

### Every Die's Job

| Die | Used for |
|:--- |:--- |
| d4 | Light weapons, first aid, backfire damage |
| d6 | Ranks 1–4, Passion spells |
| d8 | Ranks 5–6, Harm and Aid spells, martial weapons |
| d10 | Rank 7, heavy weapons |
| d12 | Rank 8 |
| d20 | Initiative, death rolls |

## Appendix B: Glossary

**Backfire.** What goes wrong when you bend the rules to make a spell work. See *Bending the Rules* and *Judging Spells*.

**Bending the rules.** Misspelling a word or bending grammar to cast a spell, at the cost of a backfire.

**Bonus action.** A quick extra action on your casting turn, only available when a feature or item allows it. See *Bonus Actions*.

**Casting pool.** The dice you roll for a school, based on your rank in it. See *Casting Pools*.

**Casting turn.** The second part of every round, when you resolve what you chose in your pre-turn. See *Casting Turn*.

**Concealment level.** How well something is hidden, from 1 to 8. You notice it automatically if your passive Reveal equals or beats it.

**Death roll.** The d20 you roll every round while downed. See *Dropping to 0 Hit Points*.

**Difficulty (DC).** The number at least one die in your casting pool must equal or beat to succeed. See *Difficulty*.

**Downed.** At 0 hit points and unconscious.

**Effect dice.** The dice you roll for damage or healing: one die per level. A spell can share them between several creatures, at least one die each. See *Effect Dice*.

**Glyph.** A written spell left on a surface that triggers later. Used by Runesmiths.

**Guard DC.** The default difficulty for actions aimed at an enemy. See *Running Enemies*.

**Known spell.** An enemy's version of a saved spell.

**Level (character).** How experienced your character is, from 1 to 12. See *Advancement*.

**Level (spell or action).** How powerful a spell or mundane action is, equal to the number of surges you rolled. See *Spell Levels*.

**Mortal limit.** The highest level a mundane action can reach: 3, unless a feature raises it.

**Mundane action.** A non-magical action, such as an attack or a persuasion attempt. It uses a school's casting pool but needs no tiles. See *Mundane Actions*.

**Party level (PL).** The average level of the player characters, rounded down. Used to build enemies.

**Passion.** The eighth school, tied to something your character loves. Its rank is your highest other rank + 2. See *Passion*.

**Passive Reveal.** Your Reveal rank, used to notice hidden things without rolling. See *Passive Reveal*.

**Pre-turn.** The first part of every round, when you choose what to do and roll your casting pool. See *Pre-Turn*.

**Prepare.** A pre-turn action that lets you counter one spell you can hear. See *Prepare*.

**Rank.** Your skill in a school, from 1 to 8. See *Ranks*.

**Resist die.** The die an enemy rolls to break free of effects and resist hazards.

**Rune.** A spell stored in an object, which anyone holding it can release. Used by Runesmiths.

**Saved spell.** A spell you've stored in a spell slot, which you can cast without tiles. See *Saving Spells*.

**Saving throw.** A roll of a school's casting pool to resist something that happens to you. See *Saving Throws*.

**Signature ability.** A special ability only your class has, such as a Spellblade's Spellfury.

**Spell slot.** A space for one saved spell.

**Subclass.** A specialization within your class, chosen at level 2.

**Surge.** A die in your casting pool that rolls 5 or higher. Each surge adds 1 to your level.

**Tiles.** The letter tiles you draw to spell your spells.

**Volume.** How loudly you cast a spell: whisper, speak or shout. It decides your spell's range and who hears it. See *Volume*.

**Ward.** A magical shield from an Aid spell that absorbs damage. See *Wards*.

## Appendix C: Example Spells
Stuck for ideas? These spells show what different words can do at different levels. They're only examples: any word that fits the school will do, and the same word can do very different things in different schools.

### Harm
- **STING.** *Level 1:* a jolt of pain hits one goblin for 1d8. *Level 3:* a swarm of stinging sparks hits three bandits for 1d8 each. *Level 6:* a cloud of burning needles fills a whole courtyard.
- **CRUMBLE.** *Level 1:* a crack runs through a wooden door. *Level 3:* a stone wall collapses. *Level 6:* a watchtower falls.
- **POISON LUNG.** *Level 2:* a knight chokes on green gas for 2d8.

### Aid
- **MEND.** *Level 1:* heal an ally for 1d8, or fix a torn rope. *Level 2:* heal two allies for 1d8 each. *Level 4:* heal the whole party of four for 1d8 each, or two badly hurt allies for 2d8 each.
- **SHELL.** *Level 1:* a ward on one ally absorbs 1d8 damage. *Level 4:* a shining dome shields four allies, each with a 1d8 ward.
- **SECOND WIND.** *Level 2:* an exhausted ally gets back on their feet, healing 2d8.

### Change
- **RUST.** *Level 1:* a lock rusts open. *Level 3:* a knight's armor turns brittle. *Level 6:* a whole gate crumbles to orange dust.
- **ICE.** *Level 1:* a puddle freezes over. *Level 4:* a river freezes solid enough to cross.
- **THE FLOOR TURNS TO MUD.** *Level 3:* the floor of a room becomes knee-deep mud.

### Move
- **GUST.** *Level 1:* knock one cultist over. *Level 3:* blow three bandits off a bridge. *Level 6:* lift a ship out of the water.
- **FETCH.** *Level 1:* pull a key across a room. *Level 4:* drag a loaded cart up a hill.
- **SWAP.** *Level 2:* you and an ally trade places.

### Control
- **HALT.** *Level 1:* a guard freezes for a round. *Level 3:* three guards stand still. *Level 6:* an entire marching column stops.
- **VINES.** *Level 1:* vines grab one creature's ankles. *Level 4:* a forest holds back a small war band.
- **LET US PASS.** *Level 2:* two guards suddenly feel you should be allowed through.

\column

### Reveal
- **GLOW.** *Level 1:* see in the dark for 10 minutes. *Level 3:* see invisible creatures for half an hour.
- **ECHO.** *Level 1:* hear the last thing said in a room. *Level 4:* hear everything said here today.
- **WHO WAS HERE.** *Level 2:* learn two clear facts about the last person to pass through.

### Create
- **SWORD.** *Level 1:* a chipped, dull blade. *Level 3:* a good, well-made sword. *Level 6:* an exceptional weapon worthy of a king.
- **BRIDGE.** *Level 2:* a rope bridge over a narrow gap. *Level 5:* a stone bridge a house could stand on.
- **LANTERN.** *Level 1:* a lantern that burns forever, but doesn't follow you around.

### Passion
These examples use the sea as a passion.

- **UNDERTOW.** *Level 1:* a wave pulls a bandit off their feet, if there's water nearby. *Level 4:* the whole harbor drags a ship back to shore.
- **DROWN.** *Level 2:* water fills a cultist's lungs for 2d6. On dry land, the DM may ask where the water comes from.
- **SALT.** *Level 1:* heal an ally for 1d6 with a sea breeze.

### Bent Spells
- **ISE SPIKE.** A frozen spike, with frostbite as a backfire.
- **FLIE.** A Move spell that lifts an ally into the air, with a buzzing swarm of flies as a backfire.
