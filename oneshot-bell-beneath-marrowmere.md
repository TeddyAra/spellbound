# The Bell Beneath Marrowmere
### A Spellbound Oneshot
</div>

## Overview
Marrowmere is a fishing village on a deep, cold lake, below high limestone cliffs. For years now, its nets have come up nearly empty, and the village is slowly going broke. Every night at midnight, the villagers hear a bell ringing from somewhere in the rock. Every few nights, someone walks out of their house in their sleep, climbs to a cliff called Gull's Leap, and jumps. Their bodies are never found.

The party is hired to find whatever is ringing the bell and put it to rest. The truth is the opposite of what the village believes: the bell is the only thing keeping them safe.

### Adventure Summary
- **Act 1: The Call to Action.** The party witnesses a sleepwalker jump from Gull's Leap, and the village reeve hires them to deal with the bell. They investigate the village and the healer's sleeping draught.
- **Act 2: Into the Weeping Cave.** The party enters the cave at the base of the cliff, finds the missing villagers digging a tunnel, solves the puzzle of the Flooded Stair, and meets the ghost who rings the bell.
- **Act 3: The Twist.** The village healer reveals that he serves the creature beneath the cliffs, and the whole village begins to walk toward the Leap.
- **Act 4: The Climax.** The party rings the bell to open the Seal and faces the aboleth, while trying to save the villagers it has enslaved.
- **Resolution.** The party deals with what's left, depending on how the climax went.

### Running This Adventure
The monsters in this adventure come from the *Dungeons & Dragons* Monster Manual. Convert them using *Using Dungeons & Dragons Monsters* in the Spellbound rulebook. The suggested tiers and encounter budgets assume a party of four level 3 characters, so a party level of 3. For a party of three or five, remove or add a minion or standard enemy to each fight.

## Background
**The Order of the Bell.** Three hundred years ago, a holy order found an **aboleth** asleep in a vast cavern under the cliffs of Marrowmere. They couldn't kill it, so they did the next best thing. They collapsed the tunnels that connected its cavern to the lake, carved a temple in front of the only remaining way in, and hung a great bronze bell there. The bell's sound keeps the aboleth asleep and breaks its hold on minds.

**The last priestess.** Over the centuries, the order dwindled. A hundred years ago, the last priestess, Sister Maelis, died alone in the temple. Her ghost still rings the bell, but only once a night, at midnight, which is all she has the strength for.

**The aboleth stirs.** Once a night isn't enough to keep the aboleth fully asleep. It can't move or break out, but its dreams reach out through the rock. They reached the **grimlocks** in the upper caves, who now worship it, and Oren Vale, the village healer, who is dying of a slow wasting illness. The aboleth promised to cure him, and to make Marrowmere rich again.

**The empty lake.** As the aboleth stirs, its mucus seeps through the rock and into the lake. Fish can't stand it. Over the past few years, they've died or fled to the far end of the lake, and Marrowmere's nets have come up emptier every season. Nobody in the village has connected this to the cliffs.

**The Weeping Cave.** The cave at the base of Gull's Leap is named for its walls, which weep a cold, clear slime. That slime is the aboleth's mucus, seeping up through cracks in the rock from its cavern below.

**The plan.** The aboleth needs hands to dig itself out. Oren collects its mucus from the weeping walls and mixes it into a sleeping draught. Whoever drinks it can be called in their sleep to Gull's Leap. They jump into the lake, where grimlocks wait in the water to drag them through an underwater opening into the Weeping Cave. There, the grimlocks pack the villagers' ears with wax so the bell can't reach them, and set them to work digging a tunnel down through the thinnest rock, toward the aboleth's cavern.

**Why no one suspects Oren.** The bell's midnight toll often wakes ordinary sleepers. Villagers believe the bell is what makes people disappear, so being woken by it terrifies them, and they go to Oren for help. His draught does genuinely help them sleep, deep enough that the bell no longer wakes them. The aboleth doesn't call everyone who has drunk the draught every night. It picks a few at a time. Most villagers who take the draught sleep soundly, night after night, and believe it works. They keep drinking it, recommend it to their neighbors, and would never suspect the kind healer who gave it to them.

**Tonight.** The tunnel is almost finished. Since Maelis's ghost attacks anything of the aboleth's that comes near the bell, including Oren and the grimlocks, Oren needs someone else to get rid of her. When the tunnel breaks through, he wants nothing left that can put the aboleth back to sleep.

## Characters
These are the people the party meets in Marrowmere and beneath it.

### Oren Vale
*The village healer, and the aboleth's servant.*

Oren is a thin, pale man in his sixties, gentle and soft-spoken, always with a satchel of little blue bottles. Everyone in Marrowmere trusts him. He's been dying of a wasting illness called Coral Choke for years. It's a common illness for the elderly that live near the sea. It slowly turns a person's lungs into a hard material until they can't breathe anymore. He often lets out dry coughs, sometimes accompanied by blood.

The aboleth's dreams promised him a cure, and promised that once it's free, the lake will teem with fish again and Marrowmere will be richer than it has ever been. It can't do either until it has been freed. He has watched his village go hungry for years, and he truly believes the creature will heal him, and that the village will be rewarded once the aboleth is free and everything is over with.

- **Wants.** To be cured, and to see the village prosper because of the aboleth.
- **Secret.** He makes the sleeping draught from the aboleth's mucus. His hands are always slightly damp.
- **Hiding it.** He's nervous and hides it well. His lies have a concealment level of 4.
- **In combat.** See *Oren Vale* under *Monsters*.

### Reeve Hadric Moss
*The village leader, and a desperate father.*

Hadric is a broad, weathered fisherman who was made reeve because no one else wanted the job. His son Tobin jumped from Gull's Leap three nights ago. He hasn't slept since, and he leans on Oren for advice. He hires the party after Oren suggested they need professionals to destroy the bell.

- **Wants.** His son back, or at least to know what happened to him.
- **Offers.** 40 gold, which is all the village could scrape together, and a family heirloom of the DM's choice to make up for how little it is.
- **Tonight.** Hadric took Oren's draught to finally get some sleep. He walks to the Leap with the rest of the village in Act 3.

### Tobin Moss
*The reeve's son.*

Tobin is a cheerful 17-year-old fisherman who couldn't sleep after a storm wrecked his boat. Oren gave him a bottle of the draught. The party finds him digging in the Warren, enthralled and deafened.

\column

### Sister Maelis
*The last priestess of the Order of the Bell.*

Maelis died a hundred years ago, alone in the temple, and her ghost has rung the bell every midnight since. She's exhausted, lonely and fiercely protective of the bell. At first, she believes anyone who comes near it serves "the dreamer below."

- **Wants.** To keep the aboleth asleep, and to finally rest.
- **Knows.** The history of the order, what the bell does, and that the Seal only opens when the bell rings. **MAKE SURE THIS IS UPDATED LATER!!!!**
- **Can become an ally.** If the party calms her, she rings the bell during the climax.

### Grandmother Ysolde
*The oldest villager in Marrowmere.*

Ysolde is nearly blind, sharp-tongued and always knitting. She remembers the stories her own grandmother told about "the bell-priests in the cliffs," and repeats an old saying: *"The bell has got to keep ringing."* Nobody else seems to know what she means, and assures the adventurers it's just nonsense and tells them not to listen to her.

### Elsie Thorn
*The sleepwalker.*

Elsie is a young net-mender whom the party sees walking toward Gull's Leap on their first night. If the party saves her, she can tell them that Oren gave her a draught for her nightmares, and that she dreamed of "something huge, with three eyes, asking for help."

## Locations
### Marrowmere
A small fishing village of about two hundred people, built along the lakeshore. The fishing has been poor for years, and many boats sit unused on the shore. Its only inn, the Drowsy Pike, overlooks the lake and the cliffs.

### Gull's Leap
A high cliff above deep water, a short walk from the village along a goat path. Sleepwalkers climb it and jump. At its base is the Weeping Cave.

### The Weeping Cave
A cave system beneath the cliffs. Its mouth is half underwater, and can be reached by boat or along a narrow ledge at low tide. Inside, it leads to the Warren, then to the old temple of the Order of the Bell: the Flooded Stair, the Bell Chapel and the Seal. Beyond the Seal lies the Dreamer's Cavern, where the aboleth sleeps.

## Act 1: The Call to Action
*About 30 minutes.*

### Opening Scene
The party arrives at Marrowmere at dusk and takes rooms at the Drowsy Pike.

The village is quiet tonight, too quiet for a fishing town. Shutters close early, and the innkeeper hurries you upstairs. From your window, the lake lies black and still beneath the cliffs.

At midnight, a bell tolls. It's deep and slow, and it seems to come from inside the cliffs themselves. As its last note fades, a door opens across the square. A young woman in her nightclothes steps out, barefoot, and begins to walk toward the cliff path.

The young woman is Elsie Thorn. Let the players react.

- **If they reach her** before she jumps, she wakes up confused, with no memory of walking. Her skin is cold and faintly slick.
- **If they don't,** she jumps from Gull's Leap. Anyone watching the water sees pale, thin arms pull her under, toward the base of the cliff, if they succeed on a Reveal action against DC 5 or have a passive Reveal of 5. Most villagers assume the lake took her.

### The Hook
The next morning, Reeve Hadric Moss gathers the village in the square. Oren Vale stands beside him.

{{descriptive
"Three nights ago, my boy Tobin walked out to the Leap. He's not the first. He won't be the last." 

The healer beside him rests a hand on his shoulder. "It's something in the cliffs. Every time that bell rings, someone walks. Please. Find whatever's ringing it, and put it to rest."
}}

Hadric offers the party 40 gold, pooled from what the village has left. He's embarrassed by how little it is, so he adds a family heirloom to make up for it.

\column

### Investigating the Village
The party can spend the morning asking around. Plant these clues, in any order.

- **Everyone who vanished took Oren's sleeping draught.** The party can learn this by asking around, with a Control or Reveal action against DC 4, or by spotting the little blue bottles in the missing people's homes, at concealment level 2.
- **Oren offers the party a bottle.** He's kind about it: "For the nerves." Anyone who drinks it makes a Control saving throw against DC 5 at midnight, or sleepwalks toward the Leap.
- **The woken have changed.** Sleepwalkers who were woken on the way have cold, slimy skin that dries out in the sun, and they feel thirsty all the time. A character who treats one notices this with an Aid action against DC 4.
- **Oren is lying.** Reading his intentions is a mundane Reveal action against his concealment level of 4. A character who succeeds can tell he's hiding something, and that he's afraid.
- **The old order.** Grandmother Ysolde tells the party about the bell-priests, and their saying: *"The bell has got to keep ringing."*
- **Empty nets.** Fishers complain that the fish started disappearing a few years ago. The few they still catch near Gull's Leap come up sickly, with a thin film of slime on their scales.
- **The Weeping Cave.** Villagers know about the cave at the base of the Leap. Nobody goes in there, because its walls drip a slimy substance. It can be reached by boat, or at low tide in the afternoon along a narrow ledge, with a Move action against DC 3.

## Act 2: Into the Weeping Cave
*About 100 minutes.*

### The Weeping Cave
{{descriptive
The cave mouth is low and wet, and the lake laps at its floor. The walls glisten with a cold, clear slime that drips slowly from the ceiling, like tears. Somewhere ahead, in the dark, something clicks and shuffles.
}}

**Encounter.** Grimlocks guard the entrance, with gricks lurking in the ceiling. For four players, use 4 grimlocks and 2 gricks, a fair fight.

**Blind hunters.** Grimlocks are blind and hunt by sound. They always know where any creature is that spoke or shouted a spell in the last round. A whispered spell only gives a character away to grimlocks within 5 feet.

**Clue.** A clay jar half full of slime stands under a dripping wall, with a stack of Oren's blue bottles beside it.

### The Warren
{{descriptive
The passage opens into a wide cave, lit by a single guttering lantern. A dozen villagers in torn nightclothes are digging at the far wall with picks and bare hands. None of them look up. The tunnel they've dug slopes steeply down into the dark, and the walls around it weep slime faster than anywhere else.
}}

Tobin Moss is among the diggers.

**The thralls.** The villagers are enthralled and deafened: their ears are packed with wax. They ignore the party unless someone stops them digging, and then they fight back. The party has to be careful not to kill them.

**Encounter.** 3 grimlocks guard the thralls. Together with the 3 or 4 thralls that join in, this is a messy, fair fight.

**Waking them.** Removing a thrall's wax takes a mundane action next to them, but that alone isn't enough. A thrall without wax is still enthralled, and just stares at nothing. They'll only wake when they hear the bell. This should tell the party what they need to do.

**The tunnel.** A Reveal action against DC 4, or Deepcant's Stone Echo, shows a huge open space only a few feet below the end of the tunnel. Tonight's digging will finish it.

**Clue.** One of the less enthralled villagers mumbles: "The healer said it would make us well. The dreamer just needs more hands."

### The Flooded Stair
Beyond the Warren, a carved stone archway leads into the old temple of the Order of the Bell.

{{descriptive
A grand stairway, carved with waves and bells, descends into a tall hall. Halfway down, the stairs disappear into black water. At the far end of the hall, beneath the surface, you can just make out the shape of a bronze door.

On the landing stand three bronze wheels, green with age, each carved with a symbol: a fish, a bell and a moon. The walls are covered with a faded mural.
}}

**The guardian.** A chuul, one of the aboleth's ancient servants, sleeps in the water.

**The mural.** The mural shows three panels: a priest rings a bell while waves go still; the moon rises; the hall stands dry.

**How the wheels work.**
- **Fish wheel.** It opens the inlet from the lake, so water floods in. The hall fills up to the landing, and the chuul wakes.
- **Bell wheel.** It closes the inlet from the lake.
- **Moon wheel.** It opens a drain to a cistern far below.

**The solution.** Turn the bell wheel, then the moon wheel, as the mural shows. The hall drains in a minute, and the chuul is left stranded on the mud floor. If the party fights it there, its Guard DC is 1 lower, and it can't use its tentacles' reach while it's struggling in the mud. It may just slither down the drain.

**Getting it wrong.**
- **Moon first.** The lake keeps refilling the hall, so the water only drops to waist height, and the chuul wakes in the water.
- **Fish at any point.** The hall floods to the landing, and the chuul attacks.

{{note
##### Other Ways Through
Let players solve this with magic. Some solutions the DM might see:
- **Freezing a path** across the water is a level 2 Change spell. Freezing the whole hall is about level 4.
- **Parting the water** is a Move spell of level 3 or higher.
- **Deepcant's Stone Echo** reveals where each wheel's pipe leads.
- **A Reveal action on the mechanism**, a mundane action against DC 4, shows which pipe pulls from the lake.
- **Swimming** to the door is a Move action against DC 4. Only Drowned Tongue speakers can cast spoken spells underwater.
}}

### The Bell Chapel
{{descriptive
Beyond the bronze door lies a round chapel carved from the rock, its domed ceiling so smooth that your footsteps echo back at you. In the middle hangs a huge bronze bell, green with age. Behind it stands a great round door of stone, carved with a single closed eye.

The air turns cold. Beside the bell, a woman in faded grey robes flickers into view. "You won't touch it," she whispers. "Not while I'm here."
}}

**The ghost.** Sister Maelis is hostile at first, because she thinks the party serves the dreamer below. She tries to possess one of them, who resists with a Control saving throw.

**Calming her.** The party can talk her down with a mundane Control action against DC 5, speak with her in the Hollow Tongue, or use Death's Ear to notice that she isn't trying to kill anyone. The party could also fight and destroy her, which is exactly what Oren wants.

### Maelis's Story
Once calm, Maelis tells the party about the Order of the Bell, the aboleth, and the Seal.

{{descriptive
"The bell keeps it asleep," Maelis says. "While it sleeps, it can only dream at people. If it ever wakes, and reaches the lake, no village on this shore will be safe." She looks at the stone door. "The Seal only opens when the bell rings. We built it that way, so no one would ever go in without its protection."
}}

Maelis is horrified to hear about the Warren and the tunnel. If the party doesn't mention it, she realizes on her own that it's nearly midnight.

## Act 3: The Twist
*About 20 minutes.*

{{descriptive
Footsteps echo on the stairs behind you. Oren Vale steps into the chapel, his satchel of blue bottles over his shoulder. His hands glisten with slime, and for the first time since you met him, his eyes are clear and bright.

"It cured me," he says. "It'll cure all of them. There's no sickness down there, no hunger, no dying. Just the dream. And tonight, the whole village is coming."
}}

Oren followed the party through the cave. If they destroyed Maelis, he thanks them warmly. If they didn't, he tries to finish the job himself, and take the bell's clapper with it.

**The village is coming.** Up above, midnight has come. Most of the village, Hadric included, is walking to Gull's Leap, and the grimlocks are dragging them through the cave to the Warren. The tunnel will break through tonight.

**Encounter.** Oren fights with 2 grimlocks, a fair fight for four players. If he's about to lose, he flees toward the Warren and returns in the climax.

**Ringing the bell.** If Oren damaged the bell, the party has to fix it first. Rehanging the clapper is a mundane Change action against DC 4, or a Change or Move spell. Then someone swings the bell, a Move action against DC 4. If Maelis is still around, she can ring it instead.

When the bell rings:
- The Seal grinds open.
- Villagers on the cliff path and in the water wake up and swim for shore, since their ears haven't been packed with wax yet.
- In the Warren, the villagers' ears are still plugged, so they keep digging. With a final crash, the tunnel breaks through.

## Act 4: The Climax
*About 45 to 60 minutes.*

{{descriptive
Beyond the Seal, you step onto a high ledge above a vast cavern. A faint green glow rises from a deep pool at its heart. On the far wall, rock crumbles away, and villagers in their nightclothes climb down from a fresh tunnel, staring at nothing.

The pool stirs. Something enormous rises from the water: a body like a great fish, long tentacles trailing behind it, and three red eyes that open, one by one, and look straight at you.
}}

### The Dreamer's Cavern
Rock ledges stand around a deep central pool. The party arrives through the Seal on a high ledge. The Warren's tunnel opens on the far wall.

**Encounter.** The aboleth fights with 2 or 3 enthralled villagers and 2 grimlocks. For four players, that's 6 to 6.5 points, deadly on paper. Waking the villagers takes them out of the fight, so the fight gets easier as the party saves people. If Oren escaped in Act 3, consider leaving out the grimlocks to make room for him.

**The aboleth's goal.** The aboleth wants to reach the tunnel and the villagers, and dig its way out into the lake. If it gets into the tunnel, the party has lost. This gives the party a reason to stand in its way.

**Waking the villagers.** A villager can only be woken by the bell after their wax is removed. That takes a mundane action next to them, or a creative spell, such as a Change spell that melts the wax or a Move spell that pulls it out. In every round someone rings the bell back in the chapel, with a Move action against DC 4:
- Every villager whose ears are unplugged wakes up and stops fighting.
- The aboleth's Guard DC is 1 lower until the next round.

This splits the party between three jobs: unplugging ears, ringing the bell and fighting. If Maelis is an ally, she rings the bell every round, but the aboleth sends grimlocks after her.

**Talking to it.** The aboleth speaks the Ashen Tongue. An Ashen speaker can use Old Authority to make it listen before it attacks. Bargaining with it is possible, but it's an aboleth: whatever it agrees to, it's lying about something.

## Resolution
*About 10 minutes.*

**The aboleth dies.** The saved villagers slowly recover, and the slime on the cave walls dries up. Maelis rings the bell one last time, a long joyful peal, and fades away. The reeve pays in full, and Tobin, if he survived, thanks the party himself.

**The aboleth is lulled back to sleep.** If the party rings the bell long enough, the aboleth sinks back into its pool, and the Seal closes. The village is safe, but someone has to ring the bell, every hour of every day. Marrowmere needs a new Order of the Bell, and the party might be the ones to found it.

**The aboleth escapes into the lake.** It lives for thousands of years, and never forgets a face. Every village on the shore is in danger, and the party knows exactly what's coming.

## Appendix: Playtest Notes
This adventure is built to test as much of Spellbound as possible in one session. Note which rules come up, what slowed the game down, and every ruling you had to make up.

##### What Each Scene Tests
| Scene | Rules |
|:--- |:--- |
| Opening scene | Control saving throws against a charm, passive Reveal |
| Investigating the village | Mundane Control and Reveal actions, concealment, reading a liar |
| The Weeping Cave | Volume and hiding against blind enemies |
| The Warren | The deafened condition, nonlethal fighting, Stone Echo |
| The Flooded Stair | Solving puzzles with spells, Judging Spells, spell levels for areas, casting underwater |
| The Bell Chapel | Possession as a Control saving throw, talking down an enemy |
| The Twist | Fighting against a clock, fixing the bell with Change or Move |
| The Climax | A boss acting twice, spells that can't be countered, splitting the party between jobs, Old Authority |
